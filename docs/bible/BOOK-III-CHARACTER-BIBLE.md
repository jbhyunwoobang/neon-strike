# 0. THE VISUAL PHILOSOPHY (EXTRACTED FROM THE REFERENCE BOARD)

Before any face: the board's underlying laws, inferred and locked for character design.
Nothing below copies a design; everything extracts a principle.

1. **Silhouette precedes feature.** The board's ideation sheets test figures as flat
   shapes first. Every Eden character must read at 100 m in fog against concrete: if the
   silhouette needs a caption, the design fails. Rule: one dominant mass + one break.
2. **People are architecture's tenants.** Costumes echo the Gravity Style — verticals,
   heavy hems, mass at the shoulders or none at all — so figures look *poured into* the
   world, not pasted on it. Architectural motifs migrate into clothing per character (each
   entry names its motif).
3. **Machine-organic restraint.** Prosthetics, packs, and rigs are appliance-honest
   (Edge paints violence like forklifts — canon); nothing chromed, nothing sleek. Cables
   sag. Shells scuff at the exact corners real use would scuff them.
4. **The rationed palette carries rank and faith.** Concrete greys are the ground state;
   bone-white + safety amber = Edge; wax-canvas olive-greys + one hand-painted green line
   = Sowers; serif-black + paper = the Ledger; red belongs to Devastators and alarm only.
   A character's 10% chromatic allowance is spent deliberately (each entry states where).
5. **Wear is biography.** Board-form concrete carries pour-lines; clothing carries the
   same law — fade maps, mended tears, tool-polish on specific contact points. Artists
   must be able to answer, for any garment: *what motion made this mark?*
6. **Light finds people the way it finds naves.** One source. Faces are lit like the
   canyon image lights figures — a blade of day, everything else shadow-held. Character
   lighting conditions are per-entry canon.
7. **No imports.** No design may read as borrowed from any existing franchise, including
   visual shorthand (no hood-cape hero kits, no tactical-catalog operators, no robes-with-
   glowing-runes). When in doubt, return to the board's worker-first honesty: these are
   people dressed for labor, weather, and belief.

# 1. ASHER FORESTER — THE PROTAGONIST

## 1.1 Identity
Official name **Asher Rell Forester** (middle name new canon). Aliases: "Roy's boy" (Noor);
Wardenry radio call "Amber Six" (his old patrol designation — Wardens on the hunt for him
still use it, which he hates precisely because it still works on him). No nickname
survives contact with him; Coil's attempts ("Ash," "Lefty") die in the shop. Age 34; born
**11 March 2037**, Halden's Ford. Height **185 cm**; weight **88 kg**; dominant hand
**right** (left hand: ivory Meridian-pattern prosthetic, 2068). Occupation: Warden First
Class (2058–2070) → Sower, cell Taproot. Status: ALIVE through epilogue. Education: parish
school (Ledger-adjacent), Edge Scholar track from 14, Wardenry Academy Cadence (top decile,
marksmanship and procedure; near-failure in "discretionary judgment," an irony his file
does not find funny). Languages: Meridian standard; field-fluent Chargehand; liturgical
Ledger phrases absorbed in childhood and deliberately unpracticed. Family: Roy (d.2062),
Enna (d.2053), no siblings, no spouse (romance: none — locked).

## 1.2 Personality
Core: a proceduralist in mourning — a man whose competence was weaponized before his
conscience finished loading. Greatest strength: he finishes things; plans survive contact
with him. Greatest weakness: under stress he obeys structure — any structure — and calls
it calm. Greatest fear: the sigh (phantom-pain trigger, canon); beneath it, the deeper
one: that he is only ever the instrument of whoever writes the form. Biggest regret: the
unattended Returning, 2053; its adult echo, the 2068 report. Philosophy at start: "The
work is neutral; harm is a failure of procedure" → demolished by Act II → replaced by
authorship ("I filed a report once. I'm correcting it."). Politics: none he'd claim;
functionally a Ledger constitutionalist by the end (*The Standing Claim* is the only
document he ever calls "correct"). Religion: lapsed Ledger; he does not pray; he waters.
Technology: intimate and unsentimental — machines are honest, paperwork lies.
Architecture: reads buildings tactically first (entries, sightlines), then — as the game
progresses — historically; the shift is scripted into his barks. Memory: hoards it and
calls the hoarding "inventory." Death: bookkeeping he refuses to delegate anymore. Hope:
suspicious of the noun, committed to the verb. Humanity: believes in crews, not crowds.

## 1.3 Daily life
Wakes 05:10 without an alarm (Academy residue). Cleans and re-stows equipment before
eating; eats standing; coffee black, cut with chicory when supply is thin (provincial
habit he'd deny). One sentence to the seedling, morning, always after the equipment and
before the coffee — the day's only unarmored words. Hobbies: none he'd name; in practice,
smallwork — re-stitching canvas, truing blades, the meditative maintenance of other
people's gear left "lying around" (the cell learned to leave it lying around). Favorite
food: tinned peaches (Driftmark barter luxury; he ate them once at his mother's table).
Favorite place: any high catwalk at grey noon — half surveillance habit, half the only
church he'll enter voluntarily. Favorite object: the Vault-lid pot. Sleep: four-hour
blocks, boots findable in the dark. Reads maintenance manuals recreationally; will not
read the banned graphic novel (he appears in it; he's tried twice). Music: refuses the
cell's speaker hours, stays in the room anyway. Private ritual: on the anniversary of the
Incident he cleans the prosthetic's shell with the same three motions the Wardenry taught
for service weapons, and does not speak that day until the seedling's sentence.

## 1.4 Visual design
Silhouette: a long vertical with mass high — heavy wax-canvas Warden weather coat,
de-badged, hanging past the knee; one break at the left forearm where the ivory shell
interrupts the coat's grey (the frame's amber-and-bone allowance spent on the hand alone).
Reads at 100 m as: *a column with one pale line.* Height impression: tall, spare,
load-bearing. Hair: dark, provincial-cut short with field clippers, greying early at the
temples by Act III. Eyes: grey-green ("stillwood with something left in it" — concept
note). Face: long, wind-cut, a face built for waiting; a fine scar seam from left wrist
vanishing under the cuff. Posture: parade-straight in Act I (Wardenry set), progressively
un-squared — by the epilogue he stands like a planter, weight low and easy. Movement:
economical, corridor-trained; he moves along walls out of habit even in open field
(animation note: this is the single most characterizing trait — never center him in a
space he can edge). Clothing: layered worker kit under the coat — canvas, felted wool,
mended precisely (his mends are grid-regular; Coil's on the same coat are chaotic; the
coat becomes a two-author document by Act III — build it that way). Materials: wax
canvas, worn composite, brass fittings gone brown. Accessories: the spade (Ch.1 gift)
slung where the Academy slung the carbine; seed cases on the old magazine rig — the
Wardenry webbing repurposed literally, the whole theme in one costume decision. Footwear:
Wardenry service boots, resoled by a Driftmark cobbler with tire stock — Edge above,
salvage below. Color: concrete greys, coat's inner lining the only warmth (oxide rust —
seen when he moves fast). Lighting: he belongs to grey noon and doorway-light; cinematics
find him half in frame-edge shadow until Act III, then progressively in the open.
Architectural motif: **the colonnade** — repetition, load, interval; his costume's seams
and rig-straps rhyme with column spacing (patterning note for the artists). Instantly
recognizable by: the ivory left hand against all that grey.

## 1.5 Animation
Walk: metered, heel-quiet, interval-regular (colonnade again); he checks corners with eyes
only, head still. Run: Academy forward-lean, weapon indexed low; no flourish. Idle: he
*maintains* — checks straps, seats mags, touches the prosthetic's wrist seam with the
right thumb (the game's most-seen gesture; it reads as prayer and is inventory). Combat
stance: square, low, boring, correct. Turning: shoulder-led, whole-body, a man trained to
never show his back to a doorway. Breathing: visible only after the sigh plays — one long
exhale, hand-flex, resume. Talking: minimal head movement, eye contact held one beat too
long. Facial acting: the face is a closed door with excellent hinges — grief shows as
stillness lengthening; the animators' rule is *subtract*. Hands: the right does the work;
the ivory left is used deliberately, almost formally, until Act III when it finally moves
unselfconsciously (this progression is a tracked deliverable). Danger: he goes quiet and
economical. Loss: he stows something — always, physically, something gets put away.
Victory: he checks his people first, gear second, self never.

## 1.6 Voice
A worn baritone, dry, close-miked intimacy in the low register; provincial vowels sanded
by Academy diction, returning under stress (the coast comes back into his mouth when it
matters — a scripted, directable drift). Rhythm: clipped, present-tense, radio grammar;
average sentence 4–9 words; the daily seedling sentences are the only lines allowed to
breathe. Avoids: "sorry" (until Ch.7 — its single use is load-bearing), "hero," his own
first name. Repeats: "copy," "hold," "still here." Stress: quieter, flatter, faster.
Emotion: silence first; when he must speak, the sentences get shorter, never longer.
Laugh: one exhaled note through the nose, surprised out of him, usually by Coil or June.
Crying: never on camera; the closest is Ch.7, one held breath, off-axis. Casting
reference (descriptive): a tired documentary narrator who used to give orders — warmth
buried under procedure, register low, natural pauses; must sustain near-whisper intimacy
for the seedling lines without dropping intelligibility.

## 1.7 Narrative (locked summary + performance keys)
Biography per Canon Lock §7. Performance keys per act: Act I — a man auditioning for his
own conscience, doing penance as task-completion; play the competence honestly, the warmth
only sideways. Act II — the floor gives out (Ch.5); the register to find is not anguish
but *exposure*: a private man read aloud. Ch.6–7: grief metabolized in labor; the funeral
scene is played almost entirely in posture. Act III — authorship: the voice steadies, the
sentences lengthen slightly for the first time, the body un-squares; at the governor his
hands do the acting (ten seconds, white knuckles, the ivory hand steady while the flesh
one shakes — lock that detail). Ending/legacy: Canon Lock §7; the last "Still here."
belongs to the valley and both parents at once — direct the read at *neither*.

## 1.8 Gameplay
Combat role: the player — mid-range procedural fighter; mastery expressed as preparation
(deployables, reloads, positioning) not flair. Weapons: service carbine and sidearm
(ballistic, white/grey with amber windows per board); the re-governed **Converter**
(cascade-lash / capacitor slam / Sower's Charge — Book I §VII.2, detailed Book V); the
spade is a held melee with intentional dignity — never comedic. Movement: grounded,
weighty, wall-affine; no slide-cancel arcadeisms (legacy prototype feel to be retuned in
Sprint phase — logged). Signature systems: the phantom-pain sigh response (brief aim-sway
+ rumble — a *readable* cost, never a control lock); regrowth-defense as the covenant
verb. Strengths: sustained fights, prepared ground. Weaknesses: openings, improvisation
debuffs early (mechanical echo of the personality flaw, tuned out across acts).

## 1.9 Symbolism
Architecture: the colonnade. Geometry: vertical line, broken once. Symbol: the double
rule (he draws it in dust at Taproot — canon). Object: the Vault-lid pot. Sound: the
anti-sigh (his ending chord). Color: grey with one bone-white line. Light: grey noon,
doorway blades. Weather: dust lifting. Musical motif: the hymn's bass line only — the
melody withheld from his theme until the epilogue, where it completes over the valley
(composer directive).

## 1.10 Emotional design
First sight: *respect without warmth* — a professional you'd trust and not befriend.
Evolution: respect → suspicion (what is he not saying?) → wound-witness → kinship earned
in labor. Defining scene: the held governor. Defining line: "Still here." Defining image:
the ivory hand planting a seedling at the valley tree line — Edge's receipt, repurposed
into the first credit.

# 2. DR. BECK GRIMWOOD — THE PRIMARY ANTAGONIST

## 2.1 Identity
Official name **Beckett Immanuel Grimwood** ("Beck" since childhood; the full name appears
once, on the parish ledger page, and the middle name is his father's little joke of faith).
Aliases: "the Founder-Mind" (Edge internal), "the Reckoner's son" (provincial, never said
to his face since 2031). Age 70; born **2 November 2001**, Halden's Ford. Height **178
cm**, weight **71 kg** — a spare, upright seventy. Dominant hand: **left — pre-injury**;
the ruined mechanical left arm (board-final) forced a meticulous retraining to the right
in his forties; his right-hand writing is perfect and slightly too slow, like everything
else about him (the injury's cause is UNKNOWN in canon — Book III does not resolve it;
design note: the arm is *old* damage, industrial, maintained rather than upgraded — he
keeps it as a ledger entry against himself). Occupation: Chief Scientist, Edge Corp;
author of the Cycle, the Converter, the Devastator program, Project Evergreen. Status:
ALIVE — audited, arrested (2071). Education: parish school; Cadence Institute
(bioenergetics, youngest chair-track in its history). Languages: Meridian standard;
liturgical Ledger (native, unforgotten — he thinks in it); Chargehand (he designed a third
of its glyphs). Family: father the parish Reckoner (d. UNKNOWN), mother lost with her
grave-tree's parish in the 2013 blight's aftermath records; no spouse; no children —
"Evergreen is my estate."

## 2.2 Personality
Core: a grief that industrialized. Grimwood is what happens when a Ledger conscience
concludes the ledger itself is broken and appoints himself its replacement. Strength:
total intellectual honesty inside his premise — he never lies about facts, only about
frames. Weakness: the premise — he cannot grant that anything alive exceeds its model,
because the day the orchard died, sentiment lost and arithmetic held him together.
Fear: *unrecorded loss* — the thing that vanishes without an entry; it is why he archives,
why he funds burn wards, why the species list is recited nightly. Regret: "that the world
made the solution necessary" — never the solution. Philosophy: "planting is sentiment;
banking is love." Politics: none — states are ledgers with armies; he works with whoever
keeps books. Religion: apostate Ledger — which is to say, the most religious man in the
game; Evergreen is a Returning (locked). Technology: his native language of care.
Architecture: commissions memory in concrete (the inverted Hall). Memory: the only
immortality he accepts. Death: a filing failure. Hope: "a word for arithmetic not yet
done." Humanity: loves it the way an archivist loves a collection — completely, and only
once catalogued.

## 2.3 Daily life
Rises 04:30; tea, no food until noon (Institute habit). Reviews Evergreen intake schedules
personally, annotating in Chargehand with a drafting pencil — he distrusts styluses (paper
is the Ledger's residue in him; locked detail: "what is written in charge can be
unwritten"). Walks the chamber's colonnade twice daily, exactly, counting bays. Favorite
food: provincial barley bread, which he has flown in and eats alone, and would be
mortified to have catalogued. Favorite place: the antechamber bench facing the array —
the pew position, though he'd file the observation under acoustics. Favorite object: his
father's drafting pencil stub, brass-capped, kept in the coat's breast pocket over the
heart like a fountain pen's ghost. Sleep: five hours, unbroken, unbothered — the sleep of
a man whose conscience has excellent filing. Reads: soil chemistry, patristic Ledger
commentary (annotated in two hands, young and old), and — new canon, small and human —
seed catalogues from before the Quiet, the way other men read old love letters. Music:
the hymn, played by himself, badly, on a chamber piano at slowed tempo; he believes he is
playing it correctly. Private ritual: the species list, four hundred binomials, nightly,
aloud, alone — canon; the VO must record the full list once.

## 2.4 Visual design
Silhouette: a pale vertical with one dark ruin — the long stone-white coat (lab and
liturgy in one garment, high-collared, hem heavy as poured formwork) against the
blackened mechanical left arm; at 100 m he reads as *a lit column with a burnt edge.*
Height impression: taller than he is; the coat and stillness do it. Hair: white, swept
back, precise. Eyes: pale amber-brown behind round brass-rimmed spectacles (board-final;
the lenses catch the array's light — cinematographers use the glasses as his mask: when
we must not read him, we light the lenses). Face: fine-boned, ascetic, kind — cast and
model for *warmth*; the horror is the warmth (locked). The collar-mask (board) sits at
his throat, worn in the chamber's clean sections — a surgeon's propriety, not menace.
Posture: perfectly vertical, hands clasped behind — the right holding the mechanical
left's wrist, a self-shackling gesture that is his signature stance. Movement: slow,
frictionless, docent-calm; he never hurries because nothing he has ever decided was done
in haste. Clothing materials: dense wool-felt whites and bone-greys, brushed steel
fasteners; the arm: matte industrial alloy, cable-sagged, appliance-honest, its amber
status ring twin to Asher's hand (build the two props as siblings — locked mirror).
Accessories: the pencil stub; a flat brass case holding four hundred index cards.
Footwear: black felt-and-leather institute shoes, silent. Color: bone-white spent as his
entire allowance; the arm is his shadow made wearable. Lighting: lit from below (the
chamber's law — light rises from the array); his key light is always *wrong-side*,
gently. Architectural motif: **the aperture** — the single opening; his collar, his
lenses, the chamber's downward oculus all iterate it. Recognizable instantly by: white
coat, black arm, brass circles.

## 2.5 Animation
Walk: processional, even-tempo; he stops *completely* between movements — no idle drift
(the stillness must be engineered; it reads as authority and as taxidermy). Idle: the
wrist-clasp; occasionally the right thumb strokes the dead alloy forearm — comfort or
audit, never resolved. Talking: minimal gesture; when he does gesture it is with the
mechanical arm, deliberately — he *presents* his cost. Facial acting: micro-warmth;
smiles arrive early and stay too long by half a second. Reaction to danger: none — he
recalculates; the animators' rule: threat produces in him only *attention*. To loss: the
recitation posture (chin drops, eyes close, lips move). Victory: he tidies — straightens
a card, squares the pencil; triumph as filing.

## 2.6 Voice
A soft, unhurried tenor-baritone with provincial warmth intact under Institute polish —
the accent Asher's stress reveals is the accent Grimwood never lost, gently (the two
actors must share vowel work; direct them together at least once). Rhythm: long, balanced
clauses; average sentence 15–25 words; he speaks in completed paragraphs. Vocabulary:
stewardship terms — returning, keeping, devotion, estate; never "profit" (locked), never
"harvest" (he says *withdrawal*), never slang. Stress: tempo unchanged, volume slightly
*down* — the room leans in. Emotional scenes: the same voice with longer rests; the only
break permitted is the final recalculation (Ch.10), where the sentences fragment into
arithmetic — the actor's single unraveling, half-broken, half-liberated. Laugh: a breath,
courteous, real. Crying: none; the recitation is the crying. Casting reference
(descriptive): a beloved lecturer emeritus reading a eulogy he wrote years in advance —
bedside warmth, museum diction, unbudgeable tempo.

## 2.7 Narrative
Locked (Canon Lock §7; Book II §III). Performance keys: he is on screen rarely and *felt*
constantly — the campaign builds him from environment (marginalia, index cards, the
carved sentence) so his two scenes land as reunion. Ch.9 (feed glimpse, non-scene): a
man mid-devotion, unaware of audience. Ch.10: the docent — he narrates his life's work
*to the person destroying it* with genuine hospitality; the almost-agreement peaks when
he offers Asher the intake schedule to check his arithmetic (a locked beat: he wants it
*audited*). Arrest: he thanks the arresting officer for their patience — write it, keep
it. Legacy: franchise asset (§19).

## 2.8 Gameplay
Never fought (locked). Presence mechanics: the Evergreen encounter is his boss body —
the array (schedule-denial, not health bar; Canon Lock §16); his voice is the fight's
soundtrack, mixed *closer* than the explosions (audio law: Grimwood is always the
nearest sound). Player interaction: one walkable conversation (the antechamber — the
player may examine the cards, the piano, the pew-bench; each examination changes his
next line — the game's densest reactive dialogue node).

## 2.9 Symbolism
Architecture: the aperture / inverted Hall. Geometry: circle with a thickened lower rule
— his amended double-rule (locked). Symbol: the index card. Object: the pencil stub.
Sound: piano at wrong tempo. Color: bone-white + black. Light: from below. Weather: none
— he is interior; the sky does not touch him after 2013 (he has not stood in rain since;
new canon, small, devastating — an environmental log in Evergreen records his last
exterior exposure). Musical motif: the hymn, slowed (locked); resolves to true tempo only
at the reversal — *his* theme is corrected by the ending, not silenced.

## 2.10 Emotional design
First sight: *relief* — after ten hours of machines, a kind human voice; the design
weaponizes the player's loneliness. Evolution: relief → persuasion → vertigo (he might be
right) → grief *for* him. Defining scene: the recitation overheard. Defining line: "You
cannot save the forest. You can save what the forest was for." Defining image: the white
coat kneeling at the drained array, beginning to count again.

# 3. COMMANDER EDDA HARROW — SECONDARY ANTAGONIST · MILITARY LEADER · THE FORMER FAMILY

## 3.1–3.3 Identity · Personality · Daily life
**Edda Marisol Harrow.** "The Commandant" (cadet slang, affectionate, forbidden). Age 61;
b. **19 August 2010**, provincial Belt town (pre-Quiet farming country — she watched it
grey out on leave rotations, one decade per visit). 174 cm, 79 kg, right-handed.
Wardenry Regional Commander, Concession III; author of half the field doctrine (locked).
Status: ALIVE — resigned mid-duel; epilogue: planting. Education: Republic army NCO
academy → Wardenry founding cohort (2050s). Family: a brother lost to Belt emigration
(estranged, alive, UNKNOWN whereabouts); the Academy's cadets, functionally — Asher
foremost, which both know and neither has ever said. Core: an order-keeper who believes
discipline is a form of mercy — the creed's author ("BETWEEN THE WORK AND HARM") and the
last person who means it. Strength: presence; rooms organize around her. Weakness: she
mistakes the institution for the crew; loyalty up is her blind side. Fear: that
restraint was never the variable (Ch.6 makes it text — locked). Regret: every eviction
she executed "cleanly." Philosophy: "You hold the line so the line holds you." Religion:
none; funerals unnerve her more than firefights (she has attended too many of her own
making, at parade rest, unforgiven). Daily: up at 04:50, range time before coffee,
inspects boots not faces ("faces lie"), writes incident reports in longhand, keeps a
cadet-roster notebook with two columns — *serving* / *gone* — and Asher's name moved in
her own hand from one to the other, twice.

## 3.4–3.6 Visual · Animation · Voice
Silhouette: a broad horizontal in a vertical world — the only main character whose mass
is at the shoulders (command mantle, bone-white composite over storm-grey duty fleece);
at 100 m: *a lintel walking.* Hair: iron-grey, regulation-cropped. Face: weathered,
square, laugh-lines long out of work. The amber command panels across her mantle are her
palette allowance; after the resignation she strips them (the epilogue figure is grey
entire — visual arc locked). Architectural motif: **the lintel** — she carries load
horizontally; costume seams run transverse. Movement: parade-certain, hands clasped at
the small of the back (the mirror of Grimwood's clasp — one holds a ruin, one holds a
rank; animators pair them). Combat: doctrine perfected — she moves like the manual
because she wrote it; the duel is Asher fighting his own muscle memory (locked design).
Voice: low alto, gravel at the edges, cadence like drill — short imperatives, longer
sentences only when teaching; the one register she cannot do is pleading. Casting
reference: a career officer eulogizing her own unit — pride and ash in equal measure.
Laugh: a single bark, real, rare. Words avoided: "sorry" (like her cadet — they share
this; the script knows). Repeats: "hold," "square it," "between the work and harm."

## 3.7–3.10 Narrative · Gameplay · Symbolism · Emotional design
Locked biography + Ch.6 duel (Canon Lock §7, §16 — outlasted, never killed; sidearm
emptied into the altar ash; resignation in the same motion). Boss design keys: no health
bar — plates shed; she counters Wardenry-taught habits (the player must *unlearn Asher*
to win — mechanical apostasy, locked); her barks during the duel are corrections, not
taunts — she is teaching to the last. Symbolism: lintel; transverse line; the roster
notebook (object); drum-snare cadence decelerating (music, locked); dusk light on smoke
(her weather — she arrives with fires she did not want). Emotional design: fear →
respect → grief; defining scene: the sidearm into the ash; defining line: "Every tree
you save costs me ten men's jobs and buys a machine that saves nothing." Defining image:
the epilogue glimpse — grey entire, kneeling in dirt, planting with both hands, doctrine
finally horizontal.

# 4. DIRECTOR COREN VOSS — SECONDARY ANTAGONIST · THE OPERATOR

**Coren Aldous Voss.** Age 64; b. **7 June 2007**, Cadence (arcology-born — the only main
character with no provincial thread; design significance: the future he protects is the
only ground he has ever stood on). 181 cm, 94 kg, right-handed. Grid engineer → Director
of Operations, Edge. Status: ALIVE; controls Edge in 2072. Family: divorced twice, adult
daughter in Cadence grid administration who does not use his surname (new canon; he
respects the choice and funds her division's budget anonymously — Voss's compassion is
always *infrastructural*). Core: continuity embodied — the man who kept the hospitals lit
(2064, locked) and has amortized that one unambiguous good across every compromise since.
Strength: he is *right about the grid*; his numbers are never wrong. Weakness: no dream —
he can price anything and want nothing, and knows it (the epilogue's warning — locked
"worse"). Fear: the dark; literally — a 2064 control-room memory he has never reported.
Philosophy: "Somebody has to sign." Visual: executive-engineer — charcoal suit cut like
duty gear, Edge amber only as a hairline tie-pin (his entire allowance in 2 mm); the only
main character who wears *no worn surfaces* — everything replaced before it can carry
biography (wear-as-biography law, inverted deliberately: Voss erases his). Silhouette at
