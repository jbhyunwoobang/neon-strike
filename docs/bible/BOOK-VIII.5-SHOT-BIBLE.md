# SECTION 1 — THE VISUAL LANGUAGE (THE PHILOSOPHY, ANSWERED)

**Why should architecture dominate the frame?** Because the world is the surviving
witness and the frame is its deposition [BVI §0]. Every composition is a testimony
hierarchy: the civilization first, its evidence second, its tenants third. A frame
where a person outranks the room is a frame from a different game.

**Why should humans appear small?** Because smallness is the argument — the tenant
under 3% IS the Accord Age [BVIII §17]. The audience must feel the pricing of a body
against a conviction; intimacy is then spent like currency (one full face per scene),
and it detonates precisely because the frames around it refused it.

**Why should silence replace dialogue?** Because the Quiet is the antagonist and
silence is its screen time [B0 §2.6]; because paperwork lies and speech can be
doubted, but a held frame cannot; and because nine of the game's twenty-five
unforgettable moments are silent [BIV.5 §16] — the portfolio proves the method.

**Why should negative space exist?** Because emptiness is content [BIV §1]: vacancy
reads as withdrawal, headroom reads as inheritance, and a frame filled for comfort is
a frame that lies about this world. Negative space is where the audience's own
feeling is given room to stand.

**Why should movement be slow?** Because movement is rhetoric and this world's
rhetoric is mass [BVIII §0.4]. A camera that hurries tells the audience the world is
light. Slowness is also trust: the eye that is given time becomes a reader, and
reading is this game's whole verb.

**Why should reveals take time?** Because awe is rationed (three clean gasps per
game **LAW**) and an unearned reveal is counterfeit currency. Compression before
release (35 mm threshold → 24 mm held wide) is the grammar of earning; a reveal
that times out is a promise broken [BVIII §0.6].

**Why should the camera feel patient?** Because it is a witness, not a performer
[BVIII]: it arrives after the world has spoken, holds still long enough to be
trusted, and leaves before the feeling does. Patience is the visual form of the
game's deepest instruction — attend.

**Why should the audience observe instead of consume?** Because consumption is the
Accord Age's sin — the habit of converting the stored past into the convenient
present [BI §IV.2]. A cinematography of consumption (fast cuts, riser-whoosh,
coverage buffets) would rebuild the thing the game indicts. Observation is the
counter-economy: the frame gives slowly, the audience banks meaning, and the
account compounds. We shoot the way the Ledger plants.

# SECTION 2 — THE CAMERA RULES (THE OFFICIAL HANDBOOK)

*Extends BVIII §1/§6 (binding). Format: rule → USE WHEN → NEVER WHEN.*

- **Camera height:** default = eye level of the subject's dignity — 1.6 m standing
  scenes, tabletop for Sela's icon grammar **LAW**, June's eyeline for June **LAW**.
  NEVER: arbitrary "cool" heights; hip-cam swagger.
- **Camera distance:** determined by testimony order (§1): the room's read precedes
  the face's read. Two-shot distances keep architecture legible between speakers
  **LAW**. NEVER closer than 50 mm portrait distance except the three authored
  shocks.
- **Lens philosophy / FOV:** the four-lens moral scale [BVIII §1]; gameplay FOV
  70/74 **LAW**; cinematics lens-true. NEVER: focal animation mid-shot; fifth
  focals (§6).
- **Eye level:** the human default — the camera as fellow tenant. NEVER for
  machines (they get low 35 mm from cover height — the tactical eye [BVIII §2.3]).
- **Overhead:** forbidden as spectacle; permitted ONLY as the Ledger's audit view —
  twice: the parish ledger page insert (Ch.6 R3) and the valley's epilogue pan
  head-tilt (the world's account seen at last) **[NEW CANON — the two overheads]**.
  NEVER: combat, reveals, drone-establishers.
- **Low angle:** mass-reading (tilts up ≤8°/s read inheritance); boss appliances
  from cover height. NEVER: heroizing a person — no one in this game is shot from
  below except Grimwood, whose lit-from-below symmetry is HIS law and his warning
  **LAW**.
- **High angle:** cost-reading (tilts down read consequence: the wreck, the burned
  rows). NEVER: contempt framing of any character — the game has no cartoons
  **LAW**.
- **Wide angle (24):** the world's size; reveals; insignificance. NEVER: faces
  (distortion is mockery) — except Coil's 24 mm shop context, her locked exception
  [BIII §17].
- **Telephoto (85):** judgment, distance, thresholds; Voss **LAW**; epilogue
  vignettes (witnessed from respect). NEVER: action coverage; voyeur close-ups of
  grief (grief gets 50 mm patience or handheld interiority, never a long lens's
  safety).
- **Macro:** documents, materials, the pressed leaf, hands — THE FILED TRUTH's
  instrument. NEVER: gore (none exists), weapon-porn detailing.
- **Tracking:** lateral accompaniment of processions only. NEVER leads a subject
  (the camera does not know the future).
- **Orbit:** FORBIDDEN **LAW** [BVIII §1]. No exceptions. An orbit says the subject
  is an object on display; nothing in Eden is on display except by Edge's curation,
  and the camera is not Edge's.
- **Static:** the default and the signature [BVIII §6].
- **Handheld:** inside grief only, recorded human takes, sign-off logged **LAW**.
  NEVER: energy, urgency, "realism."
- **Crane:** the two descents **LAW**. NEVER again.
- **Drone/aerial:** DOES NOT EXIST **LAW** [BVIII §6 no-aerial rule] — the sky
  belongs to Surveyors; an aerial frame is an Edge frame.
- **Slow push:** THE DAYLIGHT DREAD (≤0.3 m/s toward the understood). NEVER toward
  Asher before Ch.7 **LAW**; never as generic emphasis.
- **Slow pull:** leaving someone behind (Verge in the dark; Sela at her desk) — 
  the camera departs the way the Republic did [BVIII §6]. NEVER as a scene-ender
  cliché; each use is a moral statement, counted.
- **Reveal:** the locked grammar; input free; never in motion **LAW**.
- **POV/first-person:** the default cinematic camera — the player's eyes; hands
  visible; FOV gameplay-true **LAW**. NEVER: POV of enemies or machines (the
  audience never looks through Edge's eyes — the classification view is shown ON
  the world (spotlight, tags), never AS a lens) **[NEW CANON — the no-enemy-POV
  rule]**.
- **Third-person:** objective, earned, tenant-lawful (Asher off-center,
  wall-adjacent until Ch.7) **LAW**. NEVER: over-shoulder action cam, hero
  slow-mo, ability showcases.

# SECTION 3 — THE RULE OF SCALE (MEASURABLE PRODUCTION RULES)

*Numeric base: the scale canon [BIV §2 / IV-QR §3]. This section converts it to
camera-side law.*

1. **Entering gigantic rooms:** always through compression — the threshold's 35 mm
   ≥8 s walk, THEN the 24 mm held wide **LAW**; the body enters before the eye is
   given the volume (the audience must be *processed* by the building, like the
   tenant they are).
2. **Revealing towers/skyscrapers:** from below the datum, never from the air
   (no-aerial); first sighting always at maximum distance with two occluding
   depth-planes (the skyline's three-depth law); the tower is EARNED by approach
   across ≥2 sightings before full-frame arrival (Steelgate's three-chapter model
   [BVI §6]).
3. **Framing ceilings:** ceilings are the frame's weight — headroom ≥ 40% of frame
   height in monumental interiors **[NEW CANON — the 40% headroom floor]**; the
   ceiling's coffer/canopy rhyme (stillwood = turbine hall) composed identically in
   both biomes so the rhyme is legible.
4. **Megastructure dominance:** in any frame containing both a megastructure and a
   subject, the structure touches ≥3 frame edges; horizon lines sit in the lower
   third or below; the structure is never fully contained by the frame until the
   player has walked its interior (containment = comprehension = earned).
5. **Architecture hiding information:** per the incompleteness law [BIV.5 §4] —
   compositions always include one edge-of-frame question (a door ajar, light from
   an unlisted floor); the camera NEVER centers what the level wants wondered
   about.
6. **Tiny figures against monuments:** tenant <3% **LAW**; place the figure at a
   gold-section intersection, lit by the space's one source, facing INTO the
   volume (the audience reads the back of a witness, and becomes one).
7. **Measurables for QA:** tenant % annotated per board panel [BVIII §3]; headroom
   bar ≥40% in monument frames; three depths countable; one anomalous vertical per
   skyline; 4 s minimum on held wides; tilt speed ≤8°/s. A scale shot missing two
   of these returns to the board.

# SECTION 5 — COMPOSITION LANGUAGE

- **Rule of thirds:** the living default — tenants at intersections, horizons on
  the lower line; frames breathe for the living [BIII §17].
- **Centered framing:** earned by exactly four owners — Grimwood (composed
  authority **LAW**), isolation (§4.72's dead-center smallness), the covenant's
  protected thing (§4.48), and irony spent once (the plinth). Center = statement;
  audit every use.
- **Architectural symmetry:** power's grammar (Edge civic, the inverted nave);
  **asymmetry:** function's and resistance's honesty (industrial, Sower borrow,
  Driftmark). The camera agrees with the building it is in.
- **Leading lines:** the architecture's own axes only (colonnade ranks, rack
  aisles, rails, runnels) — lines lead because load paths lead; never paint a
  line the engineering didn't.
- **Foreground/midground/background:** the three-depth law everywhere; foreground
  = frame-cutter (fin, chain, rack), midground = subject, background = ghost;
  every plane must belong to the district kit.
- **Negative space:** ≥40% headroom in monuments (§3.3); emptiness placed where
  the feeling needs room; the GN's structural white as the same law on paper.
- **Silhouettes:** first reads at 100 m in fog **LAW** [BIII §0.1] — compositions
  test as flat shapes before lighting; the cast's fourteen silhouettes and the
  enemy families' four are the vocabulary.
- **Visual hierarchy:** testimony order (§1): world → evidence → tenant → face;
  a frame is read in that order or it is recomposed.
- **Player positioning:** entering volumes facing INTO them; wall-affine in
  corridors (Asher's trait honored by the camera); at gold sections in wides.
- **Character positioning:** per the locked per-character grammar (§4 Family G).
- **Boss positioning:** the machine inside/violating its infrastructure; Harrow
  at the axis she authored; the Array beneath the player's descent — bosses are
  placed by their ideology's relationship to architecture **LAW** [BVII §0.5].

# SECTION 6 — THE LENS HANDBOOK (SIX FOCALS, FOUR ALLOWED)

*The kit is 24/35/50/85 **LAW**. The two requested outliers are documented as
forbidden with reasons — the refusals are part of the grammar.*

- **18 mm — FORBIDDEN.** Distortion is spectacle, and spectacle is the Bright
  Hour's trade, not ours; 18 mm bends the pour, and bent concrete is a lie about
  load. Never in game, trailers, or marketing. (Its temptations — "epic scale" —
  are answered by 24 mm + distance + tenant law, which is how a witness sees big
  things: by standing back, not by warping.)
- **24 mm — the world's size.** Purpose: reveals, monuments, skylines,
  insignificance. Emotion: awe-as-inheritance. Architecture: its native lens.
  Characters: never for faces (exception: Coil's shop context **LAW**). Bosses:
  arena wides. Exploration: held wides. Dialogue: no. Trailers: opening and
  closing frames. NOT when intimacy is the subject.
- **35 mm — the human passage.** Purpose: thresholds, movement, conversation
  coverage, combat objectivity. Emotion: presence. The campaign's workhorse; the
  lens the player's own eyes approximate. NOT for judgment (too companionable)
  or icons (too casual).
- **50 mm — the patient face.** Purpose: portraits, hands, documents, icons.
  Emotion: unforced intimacy. Characters: the one-full-face-per-scene spend.
  Bosses: the power-down's witness. Dialogue: singles, rationed. Trailers: the
  emotional apex only. NOT for establishing anything — 50 mm cannot testify to
  scale.
- **85 mm — judgment and distance.** Purpose: Voss **LAW**, thresholds of power,
  the epilogue's respectful distance, the skyline ghost. Emotion: scrutiny,
  remove. NOT for grief (voyeurism), action (confusion), or any Sower interior
  (the cell is never surveilled by its own camera).
- **135 mm — FORBIDDEN.** The surveillance focal: compression that flattens
  people into targets is the marksman's optic and the Surveyor's classification
  view — Edge's way of seeing **LAW** [no-enemy-POV, §2]. The game shows that
  gaze ON the world (glints, spotlight cones, tags); the audience never looks
  through it. Never in game, trailers, or marketing.

# SECTION 7 — MOVEMENT LANGUAGE

*The four verbs [BVIII §1] applied per context; never move without emotional
purpose **LAW**.*

- **Walking camera (POV):** the reading pace — damped 0.5° during authored beats;
  the player's gait IS the dolly; most of the game's cinematography is this.
- **Exploration camera:** static-friendly — the free look does the moving; the
  world rewards stillness (Reads, holds, reveals).
- **Combat camera:** POV, honest, unassisted — no kill-cams, no slow-mo, no
  hit-pause theatrics **LAW**; the camera's combat job is CLARITY (the arena
  grain legible even under fire).
- **Boss camera:** lock-off arrivals; POV fights; the phase-boundary swaps carried
  by lighting scenarios, not camera moves.
- **Conversation camera:** statics and the Space Between; coverage minimal; when
  a scene needs punctuation, the cut is to architecture, not to a new angle.
- **Memory camera:** the GN's 2% drift and ≤4° parallax — memory barely moves,
  because it is already still **LAW**.
- **Ending camera:** pans only [B0 §11]; the epilogue is the one place the camera
  may drift like weather, because the world has finally earned ease.
- **GN transitions:** form-match both directions **LAW**; the camera never
  "enters" a page — the page replaces the world at shape-match, 150 ms stamp
  grammar.
- **Player-control transitions:** surrender on motion, return before the fade,
  ≤0.5 s to input **LAW** [BVIII §11].

# SECTION 15 — THE CINEMATOGRAPHER'S MANIFESTO

*To every camera artist who joins this project after us.*

**Every frame matters** because this franchise has staked its identity on a claim
most games are afraid to make: that one held image, honestly composed, outlasts
ten minutes of choreography. We will be recognized from a single frame or we will
not be recognized at all. Compose like the frame is going on a wall — because in
this world, what stands, remembers.

**Architecture is the main actor.** Light the room first. Block the people to the
light. When the scene reaches what cannot be said, cut to the pour and let seven
centuries of poured memory deliver the line. Your establishing shot is not setup;
it is testimony — the civilization speaking for itself before its children
interrupt.

**Silence is dialogue.** The four empty bars are the bravest cue we own. The held
frame is the bravest cut. When you feel the itch to fill — with music, with
coverage, with motion — recognize the itch as the Accord Age in you, and read the
wall instead. Hold one breath past comfortable. That breath is the game.

**Composition creates memory.** The audience will not remember what was said at
the tide-line; they will remember who stood beside whom. They will not remember
the governor's mechanics; they will remember white knuckles on a lever and light
rising like a verdict. Compose the memory first; the scene will assemble itself
around it.

**Players remember images more than exposition** — our portfolio proves it: the
ivory hand in doorway light, the ledger under the lamp, the chart falling, the
stars over the dark city, a child lowering a recorder because the world finally
sings for itself. Nine of our twenty-five immortal moments are silent. Keep the
ratio.

**This game should be visually unforgettable** not because it is loud but because
it is CERTAIN — four lenses, one light, ninety percent grey, and the discipline
to spend the rest like the last of it. Restraint, held long enough, becomes
identity. Identity, held long enough, becomes memory.

**How every shot should be judged before approval:** hold it six seconds, muted.
Ask: is it ours in one second? Does it need a word to work? Would it survive as
ink on paper? Does it keep the losses felt? Four yeses — print it.
Any no — bring the spade.
