# ECHOES OF EDEN
## BOOK IV.5 — THE EXPERIENCE BIBLE
### Edition One · The Emotional Blueprint · 2026

*Filed as Book IV.5 per the 2026-07-03 shelf ruling (formerly filed as Book V; the Art
Quick Reference is now "Book IV Quick Reference"). Governed by BOOK II.5
(Canon Lock — load first), then Books I–IV. This document designs no systems, no
characters, no lore, no visuals — it choreographs what the player FEELS, minute by minute,
and every department keys its work to it. Where earlier books locked a beat (pacing map,
silence shots, boss schemes, GN law, the four empty bars), this book cites and extends;
it never re-decides.*

**The golden rule, restated for this project:** the player must never go numb — and in
*this* game numbness is not just a pacing failure, it is a thematic one. The Accord Age's
sin is exactly that: a civilization that stopped feeling its own losses. The experience
design must therefore do to the player what the world failed to do to its citizens —
keep the losses felt. Every rule below serves that single inversion.

---

# 1. EXPERIENCE PHILOSOPHY

**Why players keep walking:** because the architecture keeps asking questions dialogue
refuses to answer. The Gravity Style is a text; traversal is reading. Players walk to
finish sentences the skyline started.

**Why they care about architecture:** because it is the only surviving witness. In a
world where paperwork lies and the countryside is dead, the pour-lines tell the truth.
The game teaches this in the first hour (the prologue's honest forest, the title's grey
noon) and rewards it forever after: attentive readers of walls are paid in
understanding — never in loot first (see §7).

**Why exploration rewards:** the reward hierarchy is fixed — (1) understanding, (2)
views, (3) human traces (letters, shrines, June's cartridge labels), (4) materiel.
Ammo is the *least* interesting thing behind any door, by design; the moment loot
outranks meaning, the Quiet has won the design meeting.

**Why silence exists:** the Quiet is the antagonist (locked). Silence in this game is
not absence of content — it is *contact with the enemy*. Every silent minute is a boss
appearance.

**Why loneliness:** the world is emptier than its architecture — vacancy as withdrawal
(locked). But loneliness here is curated to make company precious: the design starves
the player of faces so that Sela's lamp, Coil's shop, June's dock hit like meals.

**Why combat means something:** every fight defends something that can be pointed at —
a sapling, a book cart, a schedule, a door (Canon: encounters exist for story reasons
only). The player should never be able to ask "why am I fighting?" without the camera
already resting on the answer.

**Why mystery stays unresolved:** the eight locked open questions (Lock §19) are the
franchise's respiration. Resolution is spent even more carefully than green.

**Why beauty after collapse:** because the alternative is nihilism, and the Ledger is
right: grief that plants is the only grief that compounds. Beauty in Eden is always
*tended* beauty — one watered tree against a hundred-meter wall — proof of hands, not
of luck.

# 2. THE PLAYER'S EMOTIONAL JOURNEY (SIXTEEN STATIONS)

*(Duration = target dwell in that dominant state; transitions are engineered, never
drifted into. Support columns name the responsible departments' levers.)*

1. **Beginning/competence** (Prologue, 20 min): the player enjoys being good at a bad
   job. Cause: tutorial-as-procedure. Env: living forest at night-work. Music: none —
   crew chatter and rain are the score. Light: work-amber in green dark. Gameplay:
   escort verbs done well. *Transition out:* the permit prompt — two innocent button
   presses; the state ends without the player knowing it ended (the design's cruelest
   quiet).
2. **Curiosity** (Ch.1 first half, 40 min): where am I, what is this grey world? Cause:
   stillwood traversal with zero exposition. Env: the cathedral rhyme. Music: sparse
   wind-textures; the hymn's bass only. Light: grey noon. Gameplay: navigation by
   Chargehand and light. Transition: the first regrowth site — curiosity gets a verb.
3. **Isolation** (Ch.1–2 seams, ~25 min cumulative): one figure in monumental vacancy.
   Supported by the insignificance cadence (1/10 min, locked) and no-NPC stretches.
   Never longer than 25 minutes without a human trace — isolation is seasoning, not diet.
4. **Discovery** (Ch.2, 50 min): the Ledger, the orchard, the rite. The game's first
   *learning* pleasure — a faith explained by props. Music: lamplight register, the
   hymn at true tempo (Sela's reference recording).
5. **Unease** (Ch.3–4, interleaved): Driftmark's prices, the Furrows' wrong green
   (flat light — locked violation), background permits. Unease is always *ambient*,
   never scripted-jumpscare (see §12).
6. **Wonder** (distributed; peaks Ch.5 acre + Ch.9 lobby): the sanctioned gasp. Rationed
   like green: three unqualified wonder-beats per game (acre, chart-hall, valley);
   everything else is awe *with a bill attached*.
7. **Hope** (Ch.1 dawn, Ch.5 acre, Ch.7 sapling): always manual — someone planted,
   watered, mended. Music may warm ONLY when hands are on-screen doing work (the
   composers' hope-rule).
8. **Tension** (all infiltrations): built by silence + schedule pressure, never by
   music stingers; the amber pulse (12/min, locked) is the tension instrument.
9. **Confidence** (Ch.4–5 combat mastery): the player's procedures start working;
   loadout familiarity peaks. The design *grants* this plateau so Ch.5 can knock it
   down.
10. **Fear** (Ch.5 D-2 approach; Ch.9 descent): scale + purpose-unknown, per §12.
11. **Determination** (Ch.7–8): grief converted to prep. Gameplay: the three-thread
    Charge Day work; music: the walking-pace alto line.
12. **Loss** (Ch.6, 45 min): designed at maximum restraint — no slow motion, no score
    until the last minute (locked), evacuation *verbs* carrying the grief (you carry
    books; carrying IS the mourning).
13. **Acceptance** (Ch.7 Returning, 20 min): no combat for twenty minutes (locked);
    the funeral the player attends. The controller teaches it: one shovel input,
    repeated, unhurried.
14. **Revelation** (Ch.9–10): the ladder's top rungs (R4, R5). Supported by the
    antechamber's reactive quiet and the docent fight.
15. **Catharsis** (Ch.10 governor, 10 held seconds + aftermath): the game's single
    chromatic flood (locked) + the anti-sigh completing. Catharsis is *authored* —
    a held input, not a cutscene (locked) — because release must be work too.
16. **Reflection** (Epilogue, 25 min + credits): consequences read aloud, vignettes,
    the inverted ration, one bird at +0:60 (locked). The game ends quieter than it
    began; reflection is protected by a no-objective, no-prompt epilogue UI state.

# 4. THE RULE OF CURIOSITY

**The engine:** withhold labels, exhibit evidence. The game never names what the camera
can teach. Players must generate the questions themselves — *what is that / who built
this / why here / can I reach it / why empty / what happened / what am I missing* — and
the design's job is to guarantee each question has a findable answer at the player's
chosen depth (surface read → prop read → document read → Chargehand read).

**Architecture creates curiosity** by incompleteness at the edges of sightlines: doors
ajar at the end of colonnades, light falling from floors the elevator doesn't list,
desire-line paths worn toward "nothing." Rule: every major space contains one visible
inaccessible place — and 60% of them become accessible later (the other 40% stay
questions; a world fully reachable is a world fully spent). **Lighting creates
curiosity** by promising rooms: a warm pool in cold grey reads as *someone is or was
here* — and the promise must always be honest (light never lies in Eden; only
paperwork does). **Sound creates curiosity** by asymmetry: a drip where nothing should
hold water, wind with texture where no window shows, the four empty bars before every
GN page (locked) teaching players that absence itself is a signal.

# 5. THE RULE OF MEGALOPHOBIA

Scale IS a mechanic: it prices the player's body against the world's convictions.
Frequency per the locked cadence (once/10 min traversal, every arena, every chapter
open). **First impressions:** signature spaces are revealed per the locked camera law
(threshold compression → one 24 mm wide held ≥4 s) — and the player's input is *not*
locked during reveals; awe must survive the player's freedom (Playdead principle:
the un-cut is braver). **Elevators and shafts** are anticipation instruments: the
1,100 m descent (locked) is scored by cable-song and breathing only, duration a full
90 seconds, no skip — the one mandatory slowness in the game, earned by nine chapters.
**Verticality:** looking UP is dread (mass above), looking DOWN is vertigo-hope (the
governor chamber's light rises); level design distributes both per chapter mood.
**Distant structures** persist across chapters (the skyline law's anomalous vertical)
so scale accumulates memory — the player should recognize Steelgate's silhouette from
three chapters of approach. **Under enormous ceilings** the HUD thins (UI already
minimal; in signature spaces the objective line hides entirely) — bureaucracy is
embarrassed in cathedrals.

# 6. THE RULE OF SILENCE

Music stops: at every chapter's ≥6 s silence shot (locked); through all stillwood
traversal (wind-texture only); for the full Ch.6 evacuation until the final minute
(locked); before every GN page (the four bars); at the Voss negotiation (coffee, HVAC,
nothing else); for the ten held governor seconds (the anti-sigh is diegetic, not
score). Ambient dominates: everywhere the Quiet rules — and ambient in the Belt is
*designed absence* (audio team: the stillwood bed is a studio-silent room tone with
pressure, not a nature bed minus birds). Dialogue disappears: whenever architecture
can answer (locked law); entire encounters ship bark-free where the space carries the
meaning. Footsteps matter: surface-truthful and mix-forward in monumental interiors —
the player's own body is the metronome of loneliness. Breathing audible: after the
sigh (phantom-pain response, locked) and in the descent. **Silence must never be
empty:** every silent stretch contains one moving thing (dust in a blade, water
without wakes) so the ear leans in and the eye finds the world holding its breath.

# 7. THE RULE OF DISCOVERY

No markers, ever, for optional content (locked: no minimap; wayfinding is
architectural). Discovery rewards, in fixed rank: understanding > views > human traces
> materiel (§1). The psychology: reward *attention*, not traversal — things are found
by reading light, wear-paths, and Chargehand, so the skill that levels up is
perception, and perception is the game's true progression system. Hidden
conversations: approach unannounced and characters are mid-life (Coil talking to a
machine, Mara counting crates, Pale mouthing liturgy) — the world's people exist
without the player (Book III's mandate made audible). Optional doors (Noor, June's
second listen, Verge's drawings) gate epilogue *texture*, not endings (locked) — the
design tells players early, honestly, that curiosity buys meaning, not power.

# 8. THE RULE OF COMBAT (EXPERIENTIAL LAW)

Combat interrupts exploration — never the reverse; every encounter is placed where the
story would bleed anyway (survey drones find the sapling; the raid comes because Brack
sold the route). Combat releases the tension silence built and *creates the next
question* (the D-2's serial; the Wardens' creed stenciled where you fought them).
Combat changes the environment permanently: arenas carry their scars forward (the
chart stays fallen; the orchard stays burned) — the world keeps combat's ledger.
Emotional dosage: waves = covenant (protecting something visible), duels = grief,
machines = institutional emptiness; no encounter exists "because it's time" — the QC
test for any fight: point the camera at what it defends.

# 9. THE RULE OF RELIEF

Designed rests (no enemies, no objectives, atmosphere only — the locked shelter
ration): the Taproot lamplit nest; Sela's reading bench; June's dock at lamp-hour;
the arcology laundry-line balcony (Ch.8); Noor's kitchen; the parish orchard rows;
Verge's drafting wing; the observation catwalk over the Furrows (abundance
contemplated in safety); the valley itself as the epilogue's whole-chapter rest.
Placement law: one true rest per chapter minimum, always AFTER the chapter's
emotional peak, never before (rest is digestion, not appetizer). Why they matter:
these rooms are where the player's feelings catch up with the player's hands — cut
them and the game becomes a treadmill of unfelt events; the Accord Age's error,
shipped as pacing.

# 10. THE RULE OF ARCHITECTURAL REVEALS (THE SEVEN FIRSTS)

First skyline (Ch.3, Driftmark → Cadence across the water): revealed at dusk-lamp
hour, 21:9 vista, striplight city as false dawn; music enters AFTER the hold, quiet.
First megastructure interior (Ch.4 Furrows vault): the wrongness reveal — flat light,
no shadow to hide the scale in; the player walks a full minute before the far wall
resolves. First underground (Ch.7 deep-pour recon): lantern-taught geometry, reveal
by beam sweep, not by floodlight. The shaft (Ch.9→10): §5's mandatory slowness.
First religious chamber (Ch.2 Standing Hall): smallest reveal in the game —
a human door opening onto one tree in one light; awe by inversion (the intimate as
monumental). First archive (Ch.8 Registry): reveal by SOUND first — nib-scratch heard
two corridors early. First reactor-equivalent (Ch.10 Evergreen array): lit from below,
approached from above — the only reveal where the player descends INTO the light
(the game's whole theology in one camera move). First boss arena: always the arena
before the boss (locked). Duration law: players must be free to stand and look as
long as they wish; reveals never time out, and the first input after a reveal is
always the player's.

# 11–12. THE RULES OF FEAR AND HOPE

**Fear** in Eden is comprehension arriving early: the D-2 is frightening because the
player *understands* its logic before it moves (spotlight, classification barks — the
prologue taught the grammar from the operator's side). Sources ranked: purpose-unknown
architecture > scale > isolation > silence > enemies; no jump scares, no darkness-as-
monster, no horror strings — dread is built in daylight, which is worse. The two
sanctioned fear peaks: Ch.5's cordon approach (the player walks into their own
memory) and Ch.9's descent. **Hope** is manual labor made visible (pillar law):
sunlight = the three sanctioned direct-sun events (aperture judgment, canopy, golden
hour — locked); music softens only over working hands (§2.7); reconnections are staged
in rest spaces (§9); nature survives only where tended or reversed — and the design
never fakes it: every green pixel the player sees can be traced to a hand that earned
it, which is why the valley lands.

# 13. THE RULE OF LOSS

Losses beyond death, each changing play: **loss of place** — the Standing Hall burns
and its rest-space function burns with it (the Ch.7 funeral is held in its ruin; the
game deletes a save-haven and makes the player mourn a *mechanic*); **loss of trust** —
Ch.5's revelation swaps the cell's barks and idle behaviors for two chapters (the
social air cools measurably); **loss of identity** — the prologue's Wardenry HUD never
returns; by Ch.9 the player fights bone-white silhouettes wearing their old kit (the
design makes them shoot their former self); **loss of history** — burned volumes are
gone from the lore database permanently (saved ones remain — Sela's fire-order
mattered); **loss of innocence** — the permit prompt, retroactively (R2 re-prices a
tutorial). Law: every loss must subtract something the *player* used, not just
something the story valued.

# 14. THE RULE OF BOSS ENCOUNTERS (EMOTIONAL ASSIGNMENTS)

D-2 / C3-0881 = **grief weaponized** — catharsis promised, emptiness delivered
(locked: the kill changes nothing at Edge; the player's fury meets an unaware
machine, and the echo is the lesson). Harrow = **respect** — the fight the player
wishes they could refuse; her barks teach to the end; winning feels like being
disowned. D-3000 = **awe/institution** — the work continuing without content;
fought under the falling graph; the player remembers the ARENA, by design. Evergreen
array = **acceptance** — the docent fight; the player almost agrees at peak
difficulty; the finale is not victory but authorship (the held seconds). Voss
(non-boss) = **temptation** — the walk-past must cost visible effort (input held,
not tapped). Each boss's emotion is verified in playtest by exit-interview
one-word capture; if players say "epic" instead of the assigned word, the encounter
is re-staged.

# 15. CUTSCENES, GRAPHIC NOVEL, MUSIC (EXPERIENTIAL LAW)

**Cutscenes:** ideal 45–120 s (locked list: max 4 m, ending only); dialogue density
≤50% of runtime; every cutscene contains one silence ≥5 s; player control returns on
the scene's last visual beat, never after a fade (the world resumes before the
feeling fades). Real-time only; environmental cutscenes (uncut camera in-world)
preferred wherever the frame can be surrendered without a cut. **GN sequences:** they
are the game's *memory organ* — appearing at thresholds where lived events become
record (after loss, before descent); the contrast law: gameplay is present-tense and
continuous, pages are past-tense and held — crossing between them is how the player
feels time passing. Sound crosses to paper-tone; color crosses to the two-color law;
typography crosses to serif captions (all locked); the four empty bars are never
scored over. **Music behaves like architecture** (locked ambition, made operational):
themes are structures entered and left, not moods applied — motifs attach to spaces
and people (hymn/Sela, bass-line/Asher, slowed piano/Grimwood, anti-sigh/Coil,
cartridge/June) and the score's grammar is presence/absence, not crescendo; music
never tells the player what to feel BEFORE the world has shown them why. It begins
when a meaning completes; it ends when the player should carry the feeling alone;
silence wins every tie.
