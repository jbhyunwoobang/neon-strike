# PAGE 1 — THE EXPERIENCE DNA

**Emotional identity:** grief that has somewhere to go. Melancholy + hope + awe +
reflection, arriving together. The player must never go numb — numbness is the Accord
Age's sin, and this design's job is to keep the losses felt.
**Gameplay identity:** protect what can be pointed at. Preparation over flair;
procedure becoming authorship.
**Exploration identity:** perception is the progression system. Rewards ranked:
understanding > views > human traces > materiel. No markers. Wayfinding is
architectural.
**Architectural identity:** the world is the witness. Traversal is reading; pour-lines
tell the truth paperwork won't.
**Cinematic identity:** one light, fixed lenses, stillness as style, arena before
boss, the first input after awe is always the player's.
**Narrative identity:** players assemble the truth; documents and spaces carry
exposition; silence is contact with the antagonist.
**Player fantasy:** not the hero — *the gardener with a gun*: the one person willing
to file the honest entry and stand in its cost.

**The experience in 100 words:** You were good at a terrible job, and quitting it did
not make you innocent. You walk a world that stood everything it loved in grey rows
and kept the receipts. It is enormous and it is quiet and it keeps asking what you
are willing to spend. You defend small green things under buildings that outlive
belief. You lose a church, a teacher, and an alibi. Then you hold a lever for ten
seconds and buy one valley with a city's light. It isn't enough. It is one more than
they were leaving. Still here.

# PAGE 2 — THE TEN EXPERIENCE COMMANDMENTS

1. **Keep the losses felt.** No event lands unwitnessed; no cost goes unread. Numbness
   is the enemy's win condition.
2. **The player asks; the world answers; nobody narrates.** Withhold labels, exhibit
   evidence. Exposition is a design defect.
3. **Architecture outranks everyone in frame.** Humans <3% in establishing views;
   smallness staged every 10 minutes, every arena, every chapter open.
4. **Silence is the antagonist on screen.** Place it deliberately (each chapter's ≥6 s
   hold; the four empty bars); make it full — one moving thing per silent frame.
5. **Combat defends something the camera can point at.** No fight "because it's
   time." Arenas keep their scars.
6. **Wonder is rationed like green.** Three unqualified gasps per game (acre, chart-
   hall, valley); all other awe carries a bill.
7. **Hope is manual.** Green only where hands earned it; music warms only over
   working hands; sunlight three times, by law.
8. **Rest after the peak, never before.** One true shelter per chapter; rest is
   digestion.
9. **Loss subtracts from the PLAYER'S kit,** not just the story's inventory — places,
   trust, interfaces, identities.
10. **Catharsis is authored, not received.** Held inputs, walked refusals, planted
    endings. If a payoff plays itself, redesign it.

# PAGE 4 — THE PACING GUIDE

**The rhythm law:** no block repeats its predecessor's dominant; every combat block
borders a breath. Sequence grammar: monumental → threshold → pocket → monumental.
**Quiet exploration:** never >25 min without a human trace; never <1 insignificance
beat per 10 min. **Combat:** interrupts exploration, never the reverse; releases
built tension; leaves a question and a scar. **Bosses:** emotional peaks with
assigned one-word feelings (D-2 grief · Harrow respect · D-3000 awe · Array
acceptance); playtest exit-word must match or re-stage. **Cutscenes:** 45–120 s;
≤50% dialogue; one ≥5 s silence each; control returns on the last visual beat.
**GN sequences:** at thresholds where lived events become record (after loss, before
descent); four unscored bars precede every page. **Environmental storytelling:**
every dressed space answers who/what/when/what-remains/what-was-filed. **Recovery:**
one true rest per chapter, AFTER the peak (nest, Sela's bench, June's dock, laundry
balcony, Noor's kitchen, drafting wing, Furrows catwalk, the valley). **Reflection:**
epilogue is objective-free, prompt-free, combat-free. **Reveals:** threshold
compression → one 24 mm wide held ≥4 s → player moves first; reveals never time out.

# PAGE 5 — THE RULES OF WONDER

• Reveal space before threat, always. • Never label what the camera can teach.
• One visible unreachable place per major space; only ~60% ever open. • Light
promises honestly: a warm pool always means someone is/was there. • Sound asymmetry
is a breadcrumb (a drip, textured wind, the four silent bars). • Up = dread; down =
vertigo-hope; distribute per chapter mood. • Distant silhouettes persist across
chapters — scale accumulates memory (Steelgate recognizable three chapters out).
• Elevators/shafts are anticipation instruments; the 1,100 m descent: 90 s, cable-song
+ breathing, no skip — the game's one mandatory slowness. • In signature spaces the
HUD thins to nothing — bureaucracy is embarrassed in cathedrals. • Wonder quota: three
clean gasps per game; everything else priced. • Fear = comprehension arriving early,
built in daylight; no jump scares, no horror strings, ever.

# PAGE 6 — THE RULES OF COMBAT

**Why it exists:** to defend visible things (sapling, books, schedules, doors) and to
change emotional pacing. **Frequency:** per Page 3's column — max 12 continuous
minutes anywhere; Ch.7 and E have none. **How it begins:** the world's schedule
arrives (survey drones find the site; the raid follows the sold route) — never an
ambush from nowhere. **How it changes emotion:** waves = covenant; duels = grief;
machines = institutional emptiness. **How it ends:** with a question or a cost on
camera — a serial read, a creed stenciled, a wreck that doesn't know it's dead.
**Aftermath:** check people, then gear (the protagonist's own law); the arena keeps
its scars forever. **After-feel target:** heavier and clearer, never "cleared." The
player should reload the room in memory, not in inventory. **Never becomes:** filler,
a rhythm section, a corridor tax, a power fantasy, or louder than the silence it
interrupts. QC test for any encounter: *point the camera at what it defends.*

# PAGE 7 — THE RULES OF EXPLORATION

• No markers, no minimap; navigate by light, signage voices, Chargehand, wear-paths.
• Reward rank is law: understanding > views > human traces > materiel — ammo is the
least interesting thing behind any door. • Optional doors buy epilogue *texture*,
never endings; tell players honestly: curiosity buys meaning, not power. • Characters
found off-path are mid-life (talking to machines, counting crates, mouthing liturgy) —
the world runs without the player. • Landmarks: one anomalous vertical per skyline;
three depth planes; districts identifiable by materials + light alone. • Secrets are
found by *attention* (reading light, wear, glyphs), not by wall-humping; if a secret
needs a shimmer, it's misplaced. • Lore is in-world facsimile, filed as found, never
paraphrased. • Exploration must never become: checklist tourism, icon-clearing,
loot-driven sweeping, or faster than the architecture can be read.

# PAGE 8 — THE CINEMATIC RULES

Lenses 24/35/50/85 spherical, no zooms; gameplay FOV 70/74. One dominant light
source; lens-true exposure. Dolly = procession; handheld = inside grief only;
lock-off = icons; crane = the two descents; shake ≤0.5°. Headroom oppression is
house style; frame the architecture *between* speakers. Dialogue ≤50% of any scene;
every scene holds one ≥5 s silence; architecture finishes conversations. Boss intros:
arena first, boss second; each boss keeps its locked light scheme. Reveals: 35 mm
threshold → 24 mm wide ≥4 s → player's input first. GN transitions: last panel's
shape form-matches the first gameplay frame; four unscored bars in; paper-tone
crossfade. Music: begins when a meaning completes; ends when the player should carry
the feeling alone; silence wins ties; full orchestra exists once (Ch.9). Cutscene
duration 45–120 s (4 m ending only); control returns before the fade.

# PAGE 10 — THE NORTH STAR

**What this game is:** a story about one honest entry in a falsified ledger. A man who
processed forests learns what his competence cost, and spends a corporation's banked
future to buy one green valley and a night of stars. The world is enormous, quiet,
engineered, and grieving — and the player's job, minute to minute, is to feel it and
keep walking.

**What this game is NOT:** a power fantasy; a save-the-world story; a horror game; a
loot treadmill; a lecture. Nobody here is a cartoon — not the corporation, not the
faith, not the machine that took his hand.

**Why this universe matters:** because its central question is real and unanswered —
*if survival requires spending the living world, what exactly is being kept alive?* —
and because we refuse to answer it in text. We dramatize it until the player carries
it home.

**Why players will remember it:** not the explosions. The permit prompt. The lamp-lit
ledger. Forty minutes of birdsong bought with the truth. Ten held seconds. One bird,
sixty seconds into the credits. We built a memory portfolio where nine of the top
twenty-five moments are silent — that is the identity, and it is defensible only if
every department protects it every day.

**Why every artistic decision exists:** the pour remembers, the ration spends, the
tenant feels small — and all three point at the same sentence: *keep the losses felt.*

**THE MANIFESTO.** We are making the quiet game on purpose. When you are tired and
the deadline says add noise, add markers, add a mini-map, add a monologue — read the
wall instead. Trust the player the way the Ledger trusts the planter: no innocence
required, only attention. Make the room answer. Make the silence full. Spend green
like it's the last of it, because in this world it is. And when someone asks if the
moment is big enough, ask the only question this project recognizes: *does it keep
the losses felt?* If yes, ship it. If no — bring the spade.
