# ECHOES OF EDEN
## BOOK IV — VISUAL LANGUAGE & ART DIRECTION BIBLE
### Edition One · The Visual Law · 2026

*Governed by BOOK II.5 (Canon Lock — load first), then Books I–III. The reference board is
the visual-language study; principles are extracted, never copied. This document is law
for concept, environment, character, lighting, technical, VFX, UI, and marketing art, and
for engine implementation. Where this book quantifies what earlier books described, the
numbers are new canon and are logged in PROJECT_STATE.md. Where an earlier book already
locked a rule, this book cites and does not restate authority.*

**The one-frame test.** Any single frame of this game must be attributable to Echoes of
Eden by three tells, in order of reliability: (1) the pour — board-form concrete carrying
its own construction history at monumental scale; (2) the ration — a frame that is ~90%
grey discipline with its chromatic allowance spent in one deliberate place; (3) the
tenant — a human figure or human trace occupying under 3% of a space built for something
larger than habitation. If a frame passes all three, it is ours. If it needs a logo, it
has failed.

---

# 1. CORE VISUAL PHILOSOPHY

**What every environment must produce:** awe first, smallness second, unease third,
shelter rarely and by rationed exception (Canon: BI §VI.3). The player's nervous system —
not the HUD — is the primary display device.

**Scale is theology, not spectacle.** The Gravity Style's enormity exists because mass is
memory (BI §VI.1): a civilization that feared forgetting poured itself monuments to
permanence, then hollowed out. Artists must therefore never compose "epic" for epic's
sake; every monumental frame is a *sermon by a dead congregation*, and the correct
emotional color of bigness in this game is not triumph but inheritance — the player walks
through someone else's conviction.

**Emptiness is content.** Interiors are mostly vacant because the Republic owns twice
what it inhabits (BI §VI.2). Vacancy must read as *withdrawal* — the people are not
hiding, they are gone, and the furniture that remains is arranged like museum evidence of
habitation. Rule: any interior's story is told by the ratio of built volume to inhabited
volume; set-dress the inhabited 5% with obsessive humanity and leave the 95% disciplined.

**Silence made visible.** The Quiet is the antagonist (BI §XVII.5). Its visual signature:
*stillness with structure* — dead-straight horizon lines, windless particulate hanging in
light shafts, water without wakes, the stillwood's un-rotting geometry. Every level must
contain at least one composition where nothing moves but the player, held long enough to
be noticed. Motion is a currency spent like green.

**Beauty from decay, without romance.** Weathering is never picturesque set-dressing;
it is evidence (wear-is-biography, BIII §0.5). A water stain is a roof's confession. Rust
is maintenance withdrawn — which in this world is a *budget decision*, which is a moral
decision. Artists must be able to name the negligence behind every beautiful ruin.

**Loneliness appears as misfit scale;** hope appears as *tended smallness* — one watered
tree, one lamp-lit bench, one mended coat — always small, always maintained, never
triumphant; **memory appears as stratification** — pour-lines, paint ghosts where signs
were removed, floor-wear paths to doors that no longer open, the palimpsest law: every
surface shows at least two eras wherever the camera can linger.

# 2. THE RULE OF SCALE (NUMERIC CANON)

*(Meters. Binding on blockouts. Deviation requires art-director sign-off logged in
PROJECT_STATE.)*

**Interior verticals.** Human datum: 1.8 m. Habitable-pocket ceilings (nests, shops,
parish back rooms): 2.4–3.2 m — the only spaces built to the body. Standard Accord
corridor: 6–9 m tall × 4–6 m wide (a hallway is a nave). Civic/industrial halls: 18–40 m
clear. Signature spaces (Steelgate lobby, turbine halls, Evergreen chamber): 60–120 m,
with one documented super-vertical per game (the deep-pour shaft, 1,100 m — Canon ruling
II.5 §21-C). Ceilings below 2.4 m are forbidden except punishment spaces (crawls, service
gaps) used ≤90 seconds at a time.

**Openings.** Edge civic doors: 3× human height minimum (5.4 m), single-leaf, weight
visible in the hinge hardware. Residential doors: 2.2 m (the arcology concedes the body
exactly one dignity). Ledger Standing Hall door: 2.0 m — deliberately human, the faith's
argument in carpentry (BI §VI.2). Windows: either slit (≤0.6 m wide, full height) or
monumental (≥12 m); nothing domestic-sized in Accord construction — middle-scale windows
exist only in pre-Accord provincial buildings, and the contrast is the point.

**Structure.** Pillar spacing: 8–12 m in halls, doubled ranks where BI's
"repetition-as-rhetoric" applies — pour forty where four suffice, but keep spacing
metronomic; irregular spacing is reserved for damage and for Sower retrofits. Pillar
diameter: 1.2–2.6 m interior; 4–8 m for city-scale piers. Bridges/skyways: 60–240 m
spans, width ≥3 lanes of walking even where one person crosses per day (over-provision is
the era's signature). Atria: minimum 24 m × 24 m × full height; one light aperture
(§6).

**Exteriors.** Street canyons: 30–80 m wall-to-wall, 60–200 m tall. Distance between
megastructures: 150–400 m — close enough to loom in fog, far enough to read silhouettes.
Skyline law: maximum three silhouette scales per vista (near frame-cutter, middle mass,
far ghost in haze); horizons are always interrupted by exactly one anomalous vertical
(crane, dam, monolith slab) as the eye's anchor.

**The insignificance cadence.** The player must be made physically small — a composition
where they occupy <3% of frame-equivalent space — at least once per 10 minutes of
traversal, once per combat arena (waves are fought under something enormous), and always
at chapter openings. Between these, pocket-scale relief. Pacing rule: monumental →
threshold → pocket → monumental; never two pockets adjacent, never three monuments
unrelieved. Architecture reveals power by *processing* the player: approaches are
funneled through thresholds sized for freight, past queueing furniture built for crowds
that no longer come.

# 3. ARCHITECTURAL LANGUAGE — THE ENGINEERED RULEBOOK

**Proportion & rhythm.** Gravity Style masses resolve to stacked horizontal strata
(pour-lifts of 2.4–3.6 m — the lift-line is the style's pinstripe) carried on relentless
vertical ranks. Ratio discipline: facades divide into 1:2:1 or 1:3:1 bands (base : body :
crown); crowns are always the thinnest band — the style tapers *up*, humility at the sky
and arrogance at the street. Symmetry is the default of power (Edge civic reads
bilateral); asymmetry is earned by function (industrial) or subversion (Sower retrofit);
the Ledger's Standing Halls are symmetrical but *small-doored* — dignity without
domination.

**Load logic — everything must feel engineered.** Every cantilever shows its counter-mass;
every long span shows haunches thickening toward supports; every retrofit shows its
load path (Sower nests hang from existing beams with visible slings — they *borrow*
structure, never cut it: canon ideology in statics, BI §VI.2). Forbidden: floating
masses, unsupported drama, structure that only works as image. If an artist cannot
sketch the load path, the design returns.

**Construction memory.** Board-form texture with plank-width variation (0.15–0.30 m
boards); tie-hole grids at 0.6–0.9 m spacing, rust-bleeding per §4; lift-lines slightly
proud where pours cold-jointed; aggregate exposure where decades of water ran. Accord-era
(2030s+) pours are machine-formed — tighter, colder, larger panels (2.4 m ribbons);
pre-Accord provincial work is hand-boarded and warm-toned. Dating a wall by its formwork
must be possible for an attentive player — this is the environmental historian's tell.

**Infrastructure honesty.** The Gravity Style exposes its organs proudly at service
levels and hides them ceremonially at civic levels: cable trays, drainage runnels
(every large interior floor has visible falls to channel drains — water is the old
enemy), ventilation as monumental louvre-walls, maintenance gantries as permanent
architecture. Emergency systems are the amber layer: chevroned paint, striplights,
signage per the locked livery (BI §X.3). A space with no visible maintenance access
reads as *lying* — only Voss's floor at Steelgate is designed that way, deliberately.

**Space-type kits** (each detailed per district in §8): public (queue furniture, civic
serif inscriptions, one memorial per plaza — pressed-leaf reliquaries, BI §V.3);
governmental (symmetry + daylight judgment); religious (BI §VIII: one door, one aperture,
one living tree — and the inverted variant locked for Evergreen); industrial (organ-pipe
gantries, Vault logistics, chevron discipline); residential (the softened kit: moss
planters, laundry lines between fins — domestication as resistance); research (Furrows
glass vaults; clean-room whites aging badly at the human touchpoints); transportation
(mag-halls with departure boards for cancelled lines — schedule ghosts); underground
(the deep pours: geologic concrete, condensation sheen, †1,100 m shaft).

# 4. MATERIAL LANGUAGE — AGING LAWS

*(Every material ages by its economics as much as its chemistry: maintenance in this
world is a budget line, and budgets are morality. Artists must know who stopped paying.)*

**Concrete** — the mother material. Ages by water and neglect: vertical stain-tongues
below every drip line; lime bloom at cold joints; spall at rebar lines with rust halos;
freeze-crack networks in the Belt (no one heats the provinces). Edge civic concrete is
washed annually — its aging is *controlled* (uniform patina, no biology); provincial
concrete carries lichen ghosts from when there was still lichen — now dead grey lace,
a wrenching detail: even the moss is stillwooded. **Steel** — honest laborer: mill-scale
blues where new, oxide bleeding at fastener galaxies, polished bright only at human
contact points (rails smoothed by ten thousand gloves — wear maps are choreography
records). **Glass** — Accord glass is monumental and self-tinting, ages by delamination
rainbow at edges; provincial glass is small-paned, wavy, often the only survivor of a
building's face. **Copper/brass** — the pre-Accord service metal: verdigris skins on
old roofs and Coil's wirework; brown-bright gradient on handled brass (gauge-fobs,
pencil caps). **Ceramic/composite** — Edge's body armor and machine shells: chalking
at edges (Dez's plates — BIII), UV-fade mapped by storage orientation; scuffs only at
kinematically true corners. **Stone** — reserved for the Deep Inheritance layer:
flood-platform bases, floodgate inscriptions; always the bottom stratum, always the
warmest tone in frame. **Fabric** — wax canvas (Sower skin): fade at shoulder yokes,
re-waxed patches darker; felted wool (Ledger, Grimwood): felts to stone-likeness — the
faith's clothing converges on architecture. **Plastic/rubber** — the embarrassing
materials: the Accord uses them and hides them; gaskets weep, cable jackets crack in
amber-to-grey gradients. **Dust** — the Belt's snow: settles in wind-shadow wedges,
records every object's removal as a clean ghost. **Oil** — machine confession: drip
fans under every Devastator berth. **Moss/green biology** — the rarest material in the
game; may appear ONLY where the story permits life (Furrows, canopies, regrowth sites,
tended pockets) — a texture artist placing casual moss commits a canon violation; §5's
ration applies to materials as to light. **Water** — see §6 and §13; standing water is
a mirror for architecture (the world doubled and inverted — use sparingly, at thresholds
of revelation).

# 5. COLOR PHILOSOPHY — THE CANONICAL SYSTEM

*(Locked base law: BI §XVI / Canon Lock §14 — greys ~60%, bone+amber ~15%, shadow ~15%,
one living green ≤10%, red = Devastator/alarm only. This section quantifies and
choreographs it. Hex anchors are new canon for grading and UI; textures live in ranges.)*

**Master swatches.** Concrete family: `#8A8A85 / #6E6E6A / #4A4B48` (warm-grey pours) with
Belt-dust overlay `#9B948A`. Deep shadow: `#1C1D1E` (never pure black; shadow holds
detail). Bone-white (Edge shell): `#E8E4DA`. Safety amber: `#D9A226` (chevrons, panels,
status rings). Sower olive-grey: `#5C5F52`; the hand-painted line green: `#4F7A3D`.
Living green (canopy/regrowth — the sacred ration): `#3E6B34` shifting to `#77A05B` in
direct light. Ledger black: `#1A1916` on paper `#D8D2C4`. Alarm/Devastator red:
`#A6231C`. Gold (halo/liturgy accents): `#C2A44E` (locked from BI). **Forbidden:**
saturated blues and cyans (no cyberpunk drift — locked), purples, magentas, pure white
`#FFFFFF`, pure black `#000000`, and any neon of anything. Skin tones are the one
unrationed warmth — faces are where the palette permits life unconditionally, which is
the point.

**Reservations.** Hope = the green ration, always earned, always tended. Danger = amber
escalating to red only when a Devastator or detonation enters. Religion = paper + black
+ gold at ≤2% of frame. Technology = bone + amber (Edge), verdigris + marker-green
(Sower). Memory = the warm stratum (stone, brass, pre-Accord brick) surfacing through
grey. Death = desaturation toward the stillwood's mineral grey-on-grey (death in this
world is not dark; it is *toneless*). Authority = symmetry + bone-white + controlled
patina. Decay = rust-and-stain warmth — decay is warmer than maintenance, a deliberate
inversion that makes neglect feel human and control feel cold.

**Chapter color scripts** (grade targets; full stills boards in Book VIII):
P "The Sigh": living forest greens crushed under night-work amber → white-out. ·
Ch.1 "Grey Noon": the game's greyest hour; green exists only as the seedling and the
banked sapling — single-digit pixels. · Ch.2 "Orchard": lamp amber + paper warmth; the
first gold. · Ch.3 "Driftmark": wet slate + tide-bronze; harbor lamps. · Ch.4 "Furrows":
the trap chapter — abundant WRONG green (glasshouse green reads corporate: shifted cold
toward `#5E8A6A`, evenly lit, shadowless) so the player learns to distrust unearned
green. · Ch.5 "Amber": dust ochre over stillwood grey; the regrowth acre detonates true
green into the dead palette — the game's loudest color event. · Ch.6 "Standing Hall":
night blues withheld — the fire is rendered in amber/orange ONLY (no red: the raid is
work-lit, which is worse); Sela's pages glow paper-warm. · Ch.7 "Architect": ash grey +
rain; the palette's quietest chapter; one wet green sapling. · Ch.8 "Charge Day":
striplight gold and bone — festival Edge at full charm; the cartridge scene drains all
color except June's recorder-amber. · Ch.9 "Steelgate": bone-white supremacy, amber
wayfinding, the chart's etched silver — then red enters with the D-3000 (its only
permitted dominance). · Ch.10 "Evergreen": machine-white rising from below through
near-black — then the reversal floods the chamber with the full sacred green the game
has rationed for twelve hours; the single largest chromatic release in the design. ·
E "First Credit": the inverted ration — a green world in which *grey* is now the accent;
the golden-hour finale (Noor's, locked) is the only warm-sun grade in the game.

# 6. LIGHTING LANGUAGE

**The one-source law** (locked, BI §VI.2): each space is designed around a single
dominant light idea — a blade, a pool, a rising glow — with everything else shadow-held.
Fill comes from bounce, never from a second motivated source; where two sources must
exist (work areas), one dominates by 4:1.

**Natural.** Grey noon is the world's default sun: high overcast, shadowless dust-light,
1° of warmth stolen out of it in grade. Direct sun appears only: through monumental
apertures (as *judgment* — BI's law), in the canopy chapters (fractured, moving,
alive), and in the epilogue's golden hour (once per game, locked). Moon/night: Belt
nights are indecently starred (BI §III.3) — night exteriors run cold clear `#2E3440`
skies with visible star fields; city nights never see stars until the blackout (the
narrative payoff of an entire lighting scheme: Ch.10's dark skyline under sudden stars).

**Artificial.** Edge civic: horizontal striplight (warm, low, wayfinding — commerce's
grammar), never overhead floods; downlights exist only in interrogation-adjacent spaces,
a deliberate association. Industrial: sodium-adjacent amber pools with hard falloff;
gantry-mounted, always maintenance-plausible. Emergency: the amber layer pulsing at
breathing tempo (12/min — subliminally biological). Religious: lamplight and one
aperture; flame-warm `#E5B76A`, small radius, absolute. Underground/deep pour: cap-lamp
and site-lantern (Verge's amber), darkness with *geometry* — the beam teaches the space.
Boss lighting: each boss owns a scheme (locked in BII/BIII — D-2: spotlight
classification glare; Harrow: smoke-dusk; D-3000: lobby daylight blade + red; Evergreen:
lit-from-below white → green). Volumetrics: fog and dust are structural (they carry
scale by layering silhouettes) but rationed — one volumetric idea per space; god-rays
only through story-bearing apertures, never decorative. Reflections: polished floors in
Edge civic mirror the architecture (power doubled); provincial surfaces never reflect.
Shadow density: interiors hold 40–60% of frame in readable shadow; contrast is the
game's rest state, flatness its alarm (the Furrows are lit flat — and it feels wrong,
by design).

# 7. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING — THE RULES OF EVIDENCE

**The four questions** (every dressed space must answer in props alone): who used this ·
what did they do · when did it stop · what remains. The fifth, franchise question: *what
was filed about it?* — paperwork is this world's blood-spatter; every significant space
contains its documentary residue (manifest, permit, register, ledger) placed where the
work happened.

**Placement laws.** Furniture: isolated islands in monumental volume (the red-carpet
grammar — canon), arranged by last use, not by symmetry; chairs angled to dead
conversations. Debris: falls where load paths failed, sorted by what salvagers deemed
worthless (what remains is a price list of the era's values). Blood: none decorative —
this is not a gore world; violence leaves procedure behind instead (cordon tape,
evidence tags, chalk serials — the 2068 site's grammar, locked). Documents: always
purposeful, always findable, never confetti. Machines: die mid-task and are cannibalized
in order of part value; a Converter husk with its lens cored out tells a Mulcher story.
Plants: see §4's biology law — placement is plot. Water: intrusion maps roof failure;
tended dryness maps care. Graffiti: rare, small, literate — Chargehand marginalia and
the reckoned countermark; the Republic writes more than it sprays. Warning signage: per
locked livery; its *absence* in a hazardous space is itself a story (budget withdrawn).
Memorials: the era's compulsion — pressed-leaf reliquaries, name-etched benches,
grave-tree tags; every district contains at least one uncommissioned memorial (folk
grief) and one official one (managed grief); their contrast is a standing essay.
Statues: the Accord builds few figures (its monuments are infrastructure); pre-Accord
plinths stand empty or re-purposed — a plinth holding a Vault is a complete political
cartoon, usable once.

# 8. DISTRICT LANGUAGE — VISUAL IDENTITY KITS

*(Each kit: materials · light · scale · palette allowance · signature props · signage ·
sound-adjacent notes for the audio book. Canon geography per Lock §5.)*

**STEELGATE / EDGE CIVIC.** Machine-formed pale pours, polished floors (power mirrored),
bone + amber, daylight blades through 60 m+ apertures; the etched chart; queue posts for
absent crowds; grotesk signage in tract-serial grammar; scale maximal; not one visible
cable — the district that lies (§3). Sound: HVAC hush, heel echoes at 2+ seconds.
**ARCOLOGY RESIDENTIAL (CADENCE STACKS).** Same grammar softened: warmer aggregate,
moss planters (licensed green — reads *kept*, not wild), laundry lines between fins,
door-shrines of the Bright Hour (filament circles at 2.2 m doors); striplight warmth;
signage bilingual civic/handwritten. The only district where middle-class humanity is
allowed to clutter. **CONCESSION INDUSTRIAL.** Raw pour + organ-pipe harvest gantries
aimed at the ground; chevron discipline; Vault logistics (stacks, cradles, spill
scorch); sodium pools; Chargehand everywhere — the district the player learns to read.
**PROVINCIAL / QUIET BELT.** Pre-Accord brick and hand-boarded concrete, human-scaled,
grey-dusted, under Accord viaducts that stride through without touching (BI §VI.2 —
build the contempt literally: viaduct piers land in former gardens). Middle-scale
windows, empty plinths, one uncommissioned memorial per town. Light: grey noon
unbroken. **STANDING HALL PARISHES.** Lime-warm verticals, one door (2.0 m), one
aperture, one tree; orchard rows with name-tags grown into bark; paper-and-black
palette; lamplight. The kit must survive being burned (Ch.6) and rebuilt (E) — model
all three states. **THE FURROWS.** Kilometer glass vaults, flat shadowless grow-light
(§6's deliberate wrongness), corporate-green rows in clinical racks, white catwalks;
worker shrines zip-tied to irrigation (locked); the only district with abundant green
and no life you can touch. **DRIFTMARK.** Half-tide concrete, salt-bleached timber
add-ons, verdigris everything, chain and cleat props, tide-line wrack; harbor lamps
low and warm; hand-painted signage over drowned civic serif (two eras, one wall —
palimpsest law at district scale). **UNDERGROUND / DEEP POURS.** Geologic-scale
concrete, condensation sheen, cap-lamp geometry, the 1,100 m shaft; sound becomes the
architecture. **ARCHIVE / REGISTRY.** Dove-grey stacks, paper mass as structural
element, nib-scratch quiet, filament glow the registrar no longer trusts; ladders worn
at the third rung. **ENERGY (VAULT DEPOTS).** Teal charge-lines on drum ranks, red
service latches, breathing-tempo amber pulse; the closest the world gets to a heartbeat.
**STILLWOOD (DISTRICT-AS-BIOME).** Locked rhyme (BI §VI.2): trunks as columns, canopy
as coffer, cathedral light-shafts, zero motion, zero rot, cordon-tape human residue.
Build stillwood with architecture tools, not vegetation tools — it is a building now.

# 9. PROP LANGUAGE

**Doors:** Edge — single-leaf monumental, counterweight visible, amber jamb stripe;
provincial — human, paneled, mended; Ledger — the 2.0 m humility door; Sower — borrowed
doors re-hung on slings. **Elevators:** freight-first cages with gate hardware sized
for Vaults; Verge's site lift (lantern hook, chalk tallies). **Terminals/consoles:**
Edge industrial keyboards with worn key clusters mapping their one repeated task (the
permit terminal's worn keys — locked prologue prop — is the archetype); no holograms
ever (Lock rule 6-adjacent: no unphysical displays; screens are matte, low-refresh,
amber-on-dark). **Benches:** civic queue benches with name-etch memorials; parish
benches worn to shine at prayer-grip points. **Lights:** striplight rails, gantry
sodium hoods, cap-lamps, site lanterns (Verge-pattern brass), parish oil-electric
hybrids. **Pipes/cables:** sagging catenaries with drip loops; strain reliefs; Coil's
copper re-splices — every cable run must answer *what it feeds*. **Storage:** seed
crates (ranger stencils over-sprayed with Sower line), Vault cradles, tide-lockers.
**Generators:** pre-Cycle diesel relics kept as Belt lifeboats — oily, loved,
forbidden-era machinery the provinces refuse to surrender (a standing quiet rebellion,
one per town). **Vehicles:** mag-freight units, harvest crawlers, Driftmark tide-barges;
all cab-forward, operator-visible — the era shows its drivers. **Barriers:** cast
plinth-blocks (the arcade legacy's concrete barriers re-derived), chevroned; cordon
tape + evidence tags for procedure archaeology. **The hero props** (build first, at
maximum fidelity, as style anchors): the Converter · a Bioharvest Vault · the Vault-lid
pot · Sela's ledger volume · the permit terminal · Verge's lantern · June's recorder ·
the governor lever.

# 10. GRAPHIC-NOVEL LANGUAGE (THE BANNED BOOK — FULL ART SPEC)

*(Extends locked BII §XI. The GN is diegetic: pressed pages of the in-world banned
comic; the player reads the resistance's own record.)*

**Page grammar.** Panel counts: 1–5 per page, never grids denser than 5 (the book
breathes); gutters are structural white, 8% page width minimum. Splash pages open
chapters (one per act minimum): full-bleed architecture with the human tenant <3% —
the game's establishing-shot law translated to ink. **Composition:** silhouette-first
(board ideation grammar); horizon lines ruled dead level except in the two "impossible
panels" (locked) where the hand shakes — the discrepancy IS the evidence. **Ink style:**
dry-brush structure lines over wash greys; hatching reserved for machine surfaces;
figures flat-shaped, faces minimal (dots-and-line economy) EXCEPT one face per sequence
rendered fully — the page's spent emotion (matches the green-ration philosophy in
draughtsmanship). **Color law (locked, quantified):** ink-grey `#2A2B2A` + paper
`#D8D2C4` + ONE green `#4F7A3D` ≤10% of page; red `#A6231C` permitted only in
Devastator panels, ≤5%. **Typography:** captions in the civic serif, small caps, ≤12
words; speech rare, hand-lettered; Chargehand glyphs as caption substitutes once the
player has learned them (Ch.4+) — the book teaches its reader the world's script.
**Motion/integration:** pages assemble stroke-cluster by stroke-cluster (GN-4's locked
grammar generalized): 200–400 ms per cluster, parallax ≤4° on held frames, light-shift
permitted, in-panel motion forbidden. Audio: paper texture + room tone + motif at
barest voicing; the four empty bars precede every sequence (the Artist's signature —
locked). Transitions: last panel's dominant shape form-matches the first gameplay
frame (locked precedent: window→menu). **Memory/dream sequences:** Asher's recurring
dream (BII motif) renders as GN pages *without paper texture* — floating ink on black;
the grey line's advance across the dream-forest is drawn as a rising tide-rule, one
page per act. **Historical flashbacks:** archival two-tone with the paper aged darker;
dates hand-stamped. Chapter splashes double as save-slot images (UI §11).

# 11. UI LANGUAGE

**Doctrine: two authors.** All UI belongs to one of two diegetic hands — **Edge
systems** (the world's screens: terminals, wayfinding, the prologue HUD) in industrial
grotesk, amber-on-dark, tract-serial grammar; and **the Sower hand** (the player's
post-defection HUD, menus, journal): a *kit-bag* interface — paper textures, Chargehand
icons, hand-ruled dividers, the double rule as the confirm/save mark (locked symbol).
The prologue-to-Ch.1 HUD switch is a narrative event: the player watches their own
interface defect. **HUD:** minimal; health as a thin double-rule that thins further
when hurt; ammo in stenciled counters; the grenade/equipment chips as tagged kit;
objective text in serif ONLY for chapter titles, grotesk for tasks; the sigh-response
vignettes briefly amber. No minimap (wayfinding is architectural — light, signage,
Chargehand; a Sower compass-journal replaces the map screen: hand-annotated plans
acquired per chapter). **Menus/pause:** the journal spread — left page state (gear,
seeds banked), right page memory (GN thumbnails unlocked); settings rendered as
equipment tags; save = pressing a leaf (the reliquary gesture — one per save slot).
**Lore database:** the Registry drawer — documents filed as found, in-world facsimiles,
never paraphrased summaries. **Subtitles:** paper-strip lower third, serif for spoken
liturgy, grotesk otherwise; speaker-color = faction allowance (amber Edge, green Sower,
paper Ledger). **Notifications:** stamp animations (120 ms, one bounce, dry). **Fonts:**
the two locked families only (BI §X); numerals old-style in Sower UI, tabular in Edge.
**Icons:** Chargehand-derived, 2 px stroke at 1080p, no gradients. **Accessibility:**
all color codes doubled by shape (faction chevrons/lines/rules); subtitle size to 200%;
the amber pulse capped below photosensitivity thresholds; colorblind-safe variants
shift green ration to luminance contrast (tested against the grey world — mandatory QA
gate). **Transitions:** cuts and stamps, never slides; 150 ms standard; the roulette
precedent (deceleration curves) reserved for chapter cards.

# 12. CAMERA LANGUAGE (EXTENDS LOCKED BIII §17)

Lens kit locked: 24/35/50/85, spherical, no zooms. **Environment grammar:** architectural
reveals are *withheld* — approach in 35 mm compression through thresholds, then the
space given in one 24 mm wide held ≥4 s, player small in frame (the insignificance

# 13. VFX LANGUAGE

**The no-noise law:** one particle idea per frame maximum; VFX exist to carry scale,
consequence, or the sacred — never texture. **Dust:** the Belt's medium — wind-shadow
wedges, footfall lift, grey-noon columns in apertures; hangs 20% longer than physical
(the Quiet holds its breath). **Fog:** silhouette-layering tool, three-plane rule
(§2 skyline); never obscures gameplay reads. **Smoke:** work-lit from within during the
fire (Ch.6's locked amber-only law); soft-sprite billows per established pipeline.
**Rain:** coastal intrusion events only; always wind-driven, never vertical-decorative;
provincial interiors leak on cue. **Water:** mirror-calm or violent, nothing between
(the world lost its middle weathers). **Electricity/charge:** verdance is rendered as
*liquid light* in conduit — teal-white in Edge lines, green-white in Sower reversal;
never arcs, never lightning-bolts: charge in this world flows, it does not strike.
**The sigh (cascade VFX):** color drains OUTWARD from the target — a desaturation wave
crawling bark → canopy over the cascade's duration, leaves stilling mid-motion; the
world's cruelest effect must be its quietest (audio carries it; particles are minimal
lens-drift motes pulled toward the Converter). **The anti-sigh (reversal):** the exact
inverse — color as dawn flowing outward, ≤10% frame green until Ch.10's sanctioned
flood. **Explosions:** eased fireball + ground ring + soft crown per established
pipeline; concrete spalls in plate-fragments with rebar stubs (never gravel sprays).
**Sparks:** Coil's shop grammar — brief, warm, honest. **Memory effects:** none —
memory in this game is material (strata, paper, pages), never a shader; flashback is
carried by the GN layer. A "memory glow" post-effect is a canon violation.
