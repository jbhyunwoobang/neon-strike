# ECHOES OF EDEN
## BOOK 0 — THE MASTER CREATIVE BIBLE
### Edition One · The Day-1 Handbook · 2026

*This is the first document loaded in every future session and handed to every new team
member. It contains no new canon — only the distilled identity of Books I–V.5. **Loading

# SECTION 1 — PROJECT DNA (ONE PAGE)

**Title:** ECHOES OF EDEN. **Genre:** single-player cinematic story FPS · wave-survival
combat · graphic-novel storytelling. **Form:** downloadable app; engine confirmed at
Sprint 0. **Audience:** players who finish quiet games and think about them for years —
the Control/Death-Stranding/Playdead temperament, served an FPS.

**Vision:** the quiet game, on purpose. A man who processed forests for a living is
given a plant in a pot, and it ruins him. One honest entry in a falsified ledger.

**Core fantasy:** not the hero — *the gardener with a gun*: competence turned from
instrument to authorship; the one person willing to file the honest entry and stand in
its cost.

**Emotional identity:** grief that has somewhere to go — melancholy + hope + awe +
reflection arriving together. The design's enemy is numbness (the Accord Age's own sin).
**Narrative identity:** players assemble truth from spaces and documents; nobody
narrates; both sides of the argument are almost right. **Architectural identity:** the
Gravity Style — mass as memory; the world is the surviving witness. **Visual identity:**
the one-frame test — the pour, the ration, the tenant. **Gameplay identity:** protect
what the camera can point at; preparation over flair; perception is the true
progression system.

**Core themes:** memory · inheritance · guilt-as-accounting · continuity vs. aliveness ·
the cost of permanence · hope as manual labor. **Core philosophy — the Central
Question:** *"If survival requires spending the living world, what exactly is being kept
alive?"* — permanent, never answered in text, only dramatized.

# SECTION 2 — THE TWENTY ABSOLUTE RULES

1. Keep the losses felt. Numbness is the enemy's win condition.
2. The Central Question is never answered in text — only dramatized.
3. Nobody is a cartoon: Edge believes itself, the Wardenry loves its own, the faiths
   are sincere.
4. Architecture always dominates humanity (<3% rule; insignificance every 10 minutes).
5. The player asks; the world answers; nobody narrates. Curiosity outranks exposition.
6. Silence is a mechanic — it is the antagonist on screen, placed and full.
7. Green ≤10% of frame and always hand-earned; red belongs to Devastators and alarm.
8. One dominant light source per space. Daylight judges; striplight sells.
9. Everything is engineered: load paths, drainage, maintenance — or it doesn't ship.
10. Wear is biography; every mark answers "what motion made this?"
11. Emptiness is intentional: dress the 5%, discipline the 95%.
12. Every room has historical purpose and answers who/what/when/what-remains/
    *what-was-filed* — paperwork is this world's blood-spatter.
13. Combat defends something visible and leaves a scar the world keeps.
14. Every boss is an ideology (order · institution · continuity · legacy) with an
    assigned emotion, playtest-verified.
15. Hope must always exist — small, tended, manual; music warms only over working hands.
16. Rest after the peak, never before; one true shelter per chapter.
17. Wonder is rationed: three clean gasps per game; all other awe carries a bill.
18. Catharsis is authored, not received: held inputs, walked refusals, planted endings.
19. The One Speculative License: the Grimwood Cycle and its three laws; everything else
    is grounded 2070s physics. No AGI, ever. No teleportation. No energy small-arms.
20. Nothing exists only because it looks cool — and nothing may resemble another
    franchise. When in doubt: worker-first honesty; bring the spade.

# SECTION 3 — UNIVERSE SUMMARY (TWO PAGES)

**The question made a world.** In 2031, Dr. Beck Grimwood proves a living organism's
banked metabolic energy — *verdance* — can be withdrawn in minutes ("the biosphere is a
battery… time we learned to hold the terminals"). Edge Corporation commercializes the
**Grimwood Cycle** from 2038 via the **Converter** (field harvester) and **Bioharvest
Vaults** (man-sized capacitors). It ends fossil fuels, lights the arcologies — and
drinks two-thirds of Earth's wild biomass in one generation.

**The Cycle's three laws (immutable):** (1) only living systems hold verdance — the
dead cannot be re-harvested; (2) yield scales with accumulated growth — old growth is a
reservoir; (3) reversal is possible at ~10:1 loss and accelerates growth — the Sowers'
sacrament, the regrowth events' engine.

**Timeline spine:** c.1300–1900 the Deep Inheritance (flood-country **Poured
Tradition** — "WHAT STANDS, REMEMBERS" — and the **Ledger** faith) → 1900–2020 the
First Spending (fossil century) → 2030s–40s the Retreat (coastal withdrawal; arcology
megaprojects) → 2031 the paper · 2034 Edge · 2038 first commercial Bioharvest → 2038–49
the Golden Decade (zero emissions 2044; first fenced stillwoods) → **2049 First
Concession Act** (99-year lease of wildlands to Edge; the Accord Age begins) → 2050s
the Quiet Creep (provinces empty; **Wardenry** founded; Devastator program) → **2053
the Memo** (Lange proves harvested land never recovers; filed into silence) → 2057
Rotation Reform gutted → 2064 the Verdance Crash (blackouts; Voss keeps hospitals lit;
restraint abandoned) → **2066 the Sowers** founded (*The Standing Claim*) → **2068 the
Amber Incident** (a D-2 kills three workers; Asher Forester loses his left hand saving
Dez Arno; the truth — his own permit report caused it — is buried) → 2070 Asher defects
→ **2071 THE GAME** (Project Evergreen) → 2072 epilogue: the First Credit.

**The present (2071):** the **Meridian Republic** (fictionalized N. America) — capital
arcology **Cadence** (~40M) around Edge's HQ **Steelgate**; six **Concessions** (III =
"Amber," the redwood coast, the last great living canopy); the **Furrows** (guarded
greenhouse agriculture); **Driftmark** (drowned salvage coast); the **Quiet Belt**
(dead-soil provinces) filled with **Stillwoods** — harvested forests standing grey,
rigid, unrotting: a cast of a forest. Cities blaze; the land is silent; birds are a
rumor children investigate.

**Factions:** **Edge** (energy monopoly; "the lights stay on"; bone-white + amber;
never cartoon-evil) and its **Wardenry** (enforcers; creed "BETWEEN THE WORK AND
HARM"); **the Sowers** (replanting resistance; cells; green line breaking a grey
circle; "a dead Sower plants nothing"); **the Ledger** (the old faith: life as loan,
Reckoners, Standing Halls — one human door, one aperture, one living tree; the
Returning = burial beneath a grave-tree; heresy = waste); **the Bright Hour**
(arcology faith: harvest as "translation" into the grid; sincere, never mocked);
**Mulchers** (salvage syndicates, neutral, for rent); **the Registry** (archivists
keeping true numbers — "someone has to be believed later"); the Republic itself
(sovereign tenant in its own house; Green Courts, Chartered Assembly).

**Architecture:** the **Gravity Style** — the Poured Tradition with a corporate
budget. Monumental board-form concrete; repetition as rhetoric; light rationed;
ideology legible in section (Edge looms, the Ledger stands, Sowers borrow); the
stillwood and the turbine hall must rhyme — the civilization rebuilt the forest in
concrete and called it progress. **Symbols:** the circle broken by a line (Edge's
sunrise-graph vs. the Sowers' green inversion — one geometry, two futures); the
double rule (balance struck; Grimwood's thickened variant = closed by authority); the
single tree in an aperture of light; the sigh (a cascade's descending chord) and the
anti-sigh (its ascending reversal — the franchise's hope-motif); amber = warning and
wages; the ivory hand — gift, leash, receipt.

# SECTION 4 — STORY SUMMARY

**Prologue "The Sigh" (2068, playable memory):** the player is Warden Asher Forester
on a night harvest — competent, comfortable — and files a routine permit flag (two
buttons, tutorial prompt). The flagged crew is reclassified; the D-2 engages; three
die; Asher saves Cpl. Dez Arno and loses his left hand. GN pages carry the defection.

**Act I "Debit" (Ch.1–3):** 2071. Taproot cell — Mara Okafor (who recruited him with
a gifted seedling), Coil, the first regrowth defense (the covenant verb: hold waves
around a sapling while a reversed Converter banks it). The Standing Hall and Reckoner
Sela Immen; Edge announces **Project Evergreen** ("the end of wild harvesting").
Driftmark: Brack, June, the spared enemy Dez. Dr. Ivy Lange arrives: *"Evergreen isn't
a farm. It's a funeral."*

**Act II "Withdrawal" (Ch.4–7):** the Furrows — R1: the 2053 memo (the failure was
always known). Amber homecoming — the repaired D-2 boss at the record regrowth acre;
mid-fight, Harrow reads Asher's own 2068 report on the open band — **R2: the player's
tutorial keystrokes were the crime.** Mara always knew ("Debrief's at six. Bring the
spade."). Brack sells the Hall route for his crews' amnesty; the raid breaks the
armed-in-a-Hall taboo; **Sela dies carrying the ledgers**; Dez carries her out, out of
uniform, and defects; the image becomes the banned book's most-copied page. R3: the
parish ledger — **Asher and Grimwood are sons of the same town.** Sela's Returning is
the funeral Asher finally attends; Verge gives the way in.

**Act III "Return" (Ch.8–10):** Charge Day in Cadence (Pale's true books; Dez's
schedules; the forgiveness choice at Brack's tide-line; June's cartridge played on
real air). The Battle of Steelgate — the D-3000 under the etched stock chart, brought
down by the chart itself; **Voss's deal** (cancel Evergreen for Grimwood's head;
nothing fundamental changes) refused by walking past his hand — **R4: Evergreen was
never Edge's; the corporation has no dream at all.** The 1,100 m descent. Evergreen:
an inverted Standing Hall; Grimwood the docent — **R5: Evergreen is a Returning**, his
faith's holiest rite performed sincerely on the biosphere. The governor, held ten
seconds: Asher **reverses** it — the banked reserve spent back into the land.

**Ending:** one valley regrows, disproportionate to every model; Cadence goes dark
eleven hours (two deaths, read into the court record); Grimwood is audited and
arrested by Voss's Edge — *"One valley." / "One more than you were leaving."*
**Epilogue "The First Credit" (2072):** the valley legal and loud; the Quiet still a
continent wide; vignettes per player choices; June lowers her recorder because the
dawn chorus is live; Asher plants the windowsill seedling: **"Still here."** One bird,
sixty seconds into the credits. **Emotional arc:** instrument → witness → author.

# SECTION 6 — VISUAL LANGUAGE (THREE PAGES)

**The one-frame test:** every frame attributable by (1) THE POUR — board-form concrete
carrying its construction history; (2) THE RATION — ~90% grey discipline, the color
spent once, deliberately; (3) THE TENANT — human presence <3% of a space built for
something larger. Needs a logo = failed.

**Architecture.** Gravity Style: pour-lift strata 2.4–3.6 m; facades band 1:2:1 or
1:3:1, crown thinnest; pillars metronomic at 8–12 m (irregularity = damage or Sower
retrofit); corridors are naves (6–9 m tall); civic halls 18–40 m; signature spaces
60–120 m; one super-vertical per game (the 1,100 m shaft). Doors: Edge ≥5.4 m ·
residential 2.2 m · **the Ledger's 2.0 m humility door, always.** Windows slit or
monumental — middle sizes are pre-Accord only. Load paths visible or the design
returns; Sowers hang from structure on slings, never cut. Districts are dateable by
formwork (machine-cold Accord vs. hand-boarded warm provincial).

**Materials.** Aged by economics: rust is maintenance withdrawn; budgets are morality.
Concrete stains in vertical tongues; steel brightens only at hand-contact; ceramic
chalks at true corners; wax canvas fades at yokes; dust records removals as ghosts;
**biology is the rarest material** — moss only where the story permits life. Decay is
warmer than maintenance (neglect feels human; control feels cold — deliberate).

**Color.** Anchors: concrete `#8A8A85/#6E6E6A/#4A4B48` + dust `#9B948A`; shadow
`#1C1D1E` (never #000); bone `#E8E4DA`; amber `#D9A226`; Sower olive `#5C5F52` + line
green `#4F7A3D`; living green `#3E6B34→#77A05B` (≤10%, earned); Ledger `#1A1916` on
paper `#D8D2C4`; alarm red `#A6231C` (Devastator/alarm only); gold `#C2A44E` (liturgy,
≤2%). Forbidden: saturated blue/cyan/purple/magenta, pure black/white, all neon. Faces
are the one unrationed warmth. Death is *toneless*, not dark.

**Lighting.** One dominant source per space (4:1 where two must exist). Grey noon is
the default sun; direct sun only via monumental apertures (judgment), canopies
(alive), and the epilogue's single golden hour. Edge = warm horizontal striplight
(never overhead floods); emergency = amber pulse at 12/min; religious = lamplight,
small radius, absolute; Belt nights star-indecent, city nights starless until the
blackout. Contrast is the rest state; flatness is alarm (the Furrows are flat on
purpose). Boss schemes locked: D-2 spotlight glare · Harrow smoke-dusk · D-3000
daylight blade + red · Evergreen lit-from-below white→green.

**Scale & megalophobia.** Humans <3% in establishing shots; insignificance staged
1/10 min, every arena, every chapter open; pacing grammar monumental → threshold →
pocket; skylines carry max three depths + one anomalous vertical; reveals: 35 mm
threshold compression → one 24 mm wide held ≥4 s → the player's input first; reveals
never time out.

**Weather.** Dust wedges and grey noons; rain only as coastal intrusion events;
water mirror-calm or violent, nothing between; the Quiet's stillness must be visible
(hanging particulate, wakeless water).

**Environmental storytelling.** Every dressed space answers who/what/when/
what-remains/what-was-filed. Furniture islands by last use; debris sorted by salvage
value; violence leaves procedure (cordon tape, tags, serials), not gore; two eras per
lingering surface (palimpsest law); memorials everywhere in two registers — folk and
managed — and their contrast is the essay.

**Graphic novel identity.** Two colors (ink `#2A2B2A` on paper `#D8D2C4` + green
≤10%; red only in Devastator panels ≤5%); 1–5 panels/page; silhouette-first; ONE fully
rendered face per sequence (the page's spent emotion); serif small-caps captions ≤12
words; motion between panels never inside; stroke-cluster assembly 200–400 ms; four
unscored bars precede every sequence — the Artist's signature.

**Camera.** Lenses 24/35/50/85 spherical, no zooms; FOV 70/74; dolly = procession,
handheld = grief only, lock-off = icons, crane = the two descents; shake ≤0.5°;
headroom oppression; frame the architecture between speakers; arena before boss;
Grimwood alone gets symmetry + lit-from-below + heard-before-seen; one ≥6 s silence
shot per chapter — the frame must survive it.

# SECTION 7 — EXPERIENCE LANGUAGE

**The journey:** competence → curiosity → isolation → discovery → unease → wonder →
hope → tension → confidence → fear → determination → loss → acceptance → revelation →
catharsis → reflection. No two adjacent chapters share a primary emotion; the curve
never flatlines. **Pacing:** no block repeats its predecessor's dominant; every combat
block borders a breath; max 12 continuous combat minutes; Ch.7 and the epilogue have
none; 14 designed recovery beats; one true shelter per chapter, after the peak.
**Silence:** each chapter's ≥6 s hold; stillwood traversal unscored; Ch.6 unscored to
the last minute; the four bars; the Voss negotiation; the ten governor seconds — and
every silent frame keeps one moving thing. **Wonder:** three clean gasps (the acre,
the chart-hall, the valley); all other awe carries a bill. **Fear:** comprehension
arriving early, built in daylight; scale > isolation > silence > enemies; no jump
scares ever. **Hope:** manual, small, tended; music warms only over working hands.
**Bosses as emotions:** D-2 = grief weaponized (catharsis promised, emptiness
delivered) · Harrow = respect (winning feels like being disowned) · D-3000 = awe
(remember the arena) · Array = acceptance (almost agree at peak difficulty); verified
by one-word exit interviews. **Cutscenes:** 45–120 s; ≤50% dialogue; one ≥5 s
silence; control returns on the last visual beat. **GN philosophy:** the game's
memory organ — present-tense gameplay, past-tense pages; crossing between them is how
the player feels time.

# SECTION 8 — GAME DESIGN PHILOSOPHY (FEEL, NOT SYSTEMS)

**The player should feel** competent early, complicit at hour twelve, and the author
by the end — procedure becoming authorship is the mechanical arc. **Combat feels**
grounded, weighty, prepared; mastery is positioning and readiness, not flair; waves
are covenants (defend the pointable thing); the phantom-pain sigh response is a
readable cost, never a control lock. **Exploration feels** like reading — light,
wear, glyphs; rewards ranked understanding > views > human traces > materiel; no
markers, no minimap; curiosity buys meaning, not power. **Progression feels** like
perception sharpening and trust accumulating (the cell warms; the hand learns fine
work); never like a skill-tree spreadsheet. **Mystery works** by evidence-until-
understanding (every mystery eventually self-assembles) except the eight locked open
questions, which are the franchise's respiration — resolution is spent more carefully
than green.

# SECTION 10 — THE LEVEL DESIGNER'S HANDBOOK

Scale per the numeric tables (Book IV §2 / IV.5 §3) — deviations signed and logged.
Flow: monumental → threshold → pocket; never two pockets adjacent, never three
monuments unrelieved. Exploration: one visible unreachable place per major space
(~60% open later); optional doors buy epilogue texture. Combat: placed where the
story bleeds; arenas under something enormous; arenas keep scars. Secrets: found by
attention (light, wear, Chargehand); no shimmer, no markers. Landmarks: one anomalous
vertical per skyline; persistent silhouettes across chapters (Steelgate readable
three chapters out). Sightlines: incompleteness at the edges — doors ajar, light from
unlisted floors, desire-lines to "nothing." Boss arenas: revealed before the boss;
built to their locked light schemes; the D-3000's chart must fall. Environmental
storytelling: the five questions, per room, in props. Guidance: light promises
honestly; architecture funnels; the HUD never rescues a failed space. Safe rooms: one
per chapter, after the peak (nest, bench, dock, balcony, kitchen, drafting wing,
catwalk, valley). The two mandatory slownesses: the Returning's twenty minutes; the
descent's ninety seconds.

# SECTION 12 — THE AUDIO HANDBOOK

**Music behaves like architecture:** themes are structures entered and left; the
score's grammar is presence/absence; music begins when meaning completes, ends when
the player should carry the feeling alone; silence wins ties; full orchestra exactly
once (Ch.9). Motif ledger: the hymn (Sela = true tempo reference; Grimwood = slowed
piano over grid-hum; resolves true at the reversal) · Asher = the bass line only,
melody withheld until the valley · anti-sigh = Coil's, first voiced in her shop ·
June = the cartridge (found audio) · Dez = silence, then four unresolved bars ·
the Artist = the four empty bars before every GN page (never score them). **Ambient:**
the stillwood bed is designed absence — studio room-tone with pressure, not
nature-minus-birds; arcology = HVAC hush and heel echoes ≥2 s; parish = lamp-buzz and
pages; Driftmark = chain, tide, gulls-absent. **The sigh:** the world's cruelest
sound — a long descending chord; always triggers Asher's phantom response (rumble +
one exhale) until Ch.10. **Combat:** percussion from sampled machine parts; machines
get no melody; waves keep the protected thing audible (the sapling's wind, the books'
cart-wheels) under the fight. **Bosses:** per locked schemes; Harrow = one snare
decelerating; D-3000 = the orchestra, cut dead at the collapse. **Movement:** footsteps
surface-true and mix-forward in monumental interiors — the player is the metronome of
loneliness. **Dialogue/VO:** sparse, close-miked intimacy; speech-shapes per Book III
are binding; Grimwood recorded warm, never menacing; the 400-name recitation recorded
in full, once. **Weather:** wind with texture only where life or story permits;
Belt wind is empty-handed.

# SECTION 15 — THE NORTH STAR (THREE PAGES)

**Why this game exists.** Because the question is real. Every player lives inside an
economy that spends living systems to keep the lights on, and every player has,
somewhere, filed their own permit report — the small correct action inside the large
wrong system. Echoes of Eden exists to let one person feel the whole weight of that
arrangement and then hand them a spade. Not a sermon: a rehearsal. The game exists so
that ten years later, someone waters something and doesn't quite know why.

**Why this universe matters.** It is built from one beautiful idea pushed too far —
not a villain's scheme but a solution that worked. Its corporation keeps hospitals
lit. Its faith buries people under trees and its heresiarch performs his funeral rite
on the biosphere out of love. Its resistance plants at ruinous exchange rates because
the arithmetic of hope has never closed and they do it anyway. Nothing here lets the
player outsource the problem to a monster — that refusal is the universe's entire
integrity, and it is why the ending lands: one valley, honestly priced, is worth more
than a saved world handed over for free.

**What makes it unique.** The quiet, kept on purpose, in a genre that fears it. An
FPS whose combat defends saplings and book carts. A villain the player almost joins
at peak difficulty. A tutorial keystroke that becomes the midgame's knife. A UI that
defects with its owner. A score whose bravest cue is four empty bars. A memory
portfolio where nine of the twenty-five unforgettable moments are silent. Nobody else
is making this — that is both the risk and the reason.

**What must never change.** The Central Question, unanswered. The one-frame test. The
green ration. The Cycle's three laws and the one-license physics. The no-cartoon rule.
The locked fates: Sela's fire, Harrow's survival, Grimwood alive and audited, the two
blackout deaths read aloud. The four empty bars. The Artist, unresolved. "Still here."
— first and last. And the loading order itself: identity, then facts, then craft.

**How to evaluate every future decision.** Ask, in order: (1) Does it keep the losses
felt? (2) Does it dramatize the Question or dodge it? (3) Would the world do this to
itself if no player were watching? (4) Is it the cheapest honest version? (5) Does it
pass the one-frame test / speech-shape test / exit-word test for its discipline?
Five yeses: ship. Any no: revise. A "yes, but it's cool": that is a no.

**How to reject what doesn't belong.** Gently and completely. The test for imports is
embarrassment: if the pitch needs another game's name to explain it, it belongs to
that game. The test for additions is the Ledger's: does it plant something (deepen)
or only spend attention (repeat)? The test for exceptions is the manifesto below —
exceptions to the quiet must be *earned* louder than the loud they add.

**How to know when something truly belongs.** It will feel found, not invented — as
if the world had already been doing it before the camera arrived. Its wear will have
a motion, its light a source, its paperwork a filer, its grief somewhere to go. The
people of this world will be able to use it without noticing it. And you will be able
to caption it with a story reason in one sentence, without the word "cool."

## THE CREATIVE MANIFESTO OF ECHOES OF EDEN

We are making the quiet game on purpose.

We trust the player the way the Ledger trusts the planter: no innocence required,
only attention.

We spend green like it is the last of it, because in this world it is.

We build rooms that answer and silences that are full. We let the architecture finish
the sentences. We keep both sides almost right, because that is where the question
lives.

We do not add noise when we are tired. We do not add markers when we are scared. We
do not answer the question when we are tempted. We read the wall instead.

Hope, here, is manual. Catharsis, here, is authored. Every payoff is held, walked, or
planted — never handed over.

And when any decision, any asset, any deadline compromise stands before us, we ask
the only question this project recognizes:

**Does it keep the losses felt?**

If yes — ship it.

If no — bring the spade.
