# ECHOES OF EDEN
## BOOK II — THE NARRATIVE BIBLE
### Edition One · The First Game · 2026

*This document sits under BOOK I — Universe Bible, which is absolute canon. Nothing here
reinvents the world; everything here spends it. It is written for writers, cinematic
directors, voice actors, level designers, environment artists, character artists, composers,
and marketing — the complete narrative of the first game, before production.*

*Reading order for new team members: Book I §I (the Central Question), §IV (the timeline),
§XIV (factions) — then this document straight through.*

# 0. WHAT THIS STORY IS

A man who processed a forest for a living is given a plant in a pot, and it ruins him.

That is the whole engine. Everything else — the corporation, the resistance, the machines,
the burning orchard, the blackout — is consequence. Echoes of Eden is not a story about
saving the world; Book I's world is past saving on any timeline a game can hold. It is a
story about **one honest entry in a ledger that has only ever been falsified** — about what
a single person can actually return, and what it costs, and why it matters anyway.

The player should finish the campaign and sit through the credits, and the feeling we are
engineering for that silence is precise: *grief that has somewhere to go.* Melancholy, hope,
awe, reflection — in that order, arriving together.

**The promise of inevitability.** Every major event in this campaign is caused twice: once
by the world (Book I's history grinds forward on its own schedule — Evergreen was coming
whether or not Asher defected) and once by character (each turn is someone acting exactly
like themselves). Nothing happens because the plot needs it. The test applied to every beat
in this document: *could a player, told only the setup, predict this and still be moved when
it lands?* If the answer is no on the first half, the beat was rebuilt.

# I. THE CORE CONFLICT

## I.1 The philosophical collision

Book I's Central Question — *"If survival requires spending the living world, what exactly
is being kept alive?"* — is dramatized as a collision between two people who both answer
**"the future,"** and mean opposite things.

**Dr. Beck Grimwood believes the wild is already dead.** His unpublished continental models
(the campaign's midgame revelation) show the Quiet Belt propagating through climate feedback
even if every Converter stopped tomorrow. Given that arithmetic, the last living canopies
are not a hope; they are an untended estate that will burn down in the coming collapse. His
Project Evergreen is therefore, in his own mind, an act of *devotion*: drink the last great
forests deliberately — bank their verdance, sequence and archive every genome, preserve the
totality of what the living world *was* — and after the crash that is coming regardless,
replant a designed, managed, unlosable nature from the archive. He is not harvesting the
redwood coast. He is, in his own vocabulary, **performing its Returning** — the Ledger
funeral rite of his childhood — for the entire biosphere, so that something survives to be
resurrected. *"You cannot save the forest,"* he says in the final act, gently, like a doctor.
*"You can save what the forest was for."*

**The Sowers believe a banked world is a dead world with good records.** Their evidence is
small, physical, and growing at regrowth sites across the Concessions: life, given back its
charge, does not behave like an archive being restored — it behaves like an *agent*,
surging, adapting, exceeding every model including Grimwood's. The Ledger said it for seven
centuries: life is not a quantity; it is the thing that does the counting. You cannot
resurrect wildness from a vault, because the vault is precisely the wildness removed.

**Neither side is entirely correct, and the campaign proves both wrong honestly.** Grimwood
is right that the Sowers cannot scale: the sum of every regrowth event in history is a
rounding error against continental dieback, and the story never pretends otherwise. The
Sowers are right that Grimwood's models cannot contain what living systems do when
returned — the ending's valley proves it, and the proof costs a city its light. The player
is left holding both truths, which is the point. Both sides are protecting humanity. They
disagree about what part of humanity is worth protecting: its *continuity* or its
*aliveness*.

## I.2 The physical collision

Project Evergreen gives the philosophy a clock and a map: Edge will perform the first
whole-canopy withdrawal on the redwood coast of Concession III — Amber — the ground Asher
Forester spent six years enforcing and one night bleeding into. The campaign is the race
along that clock, and every act ends with the clock winning: Act I ends with Evergreen
announced, Act II with Evergreen armed, Act III with Evergreen *running* — and the finale is
not preventing the machine but deciding, hand on the polarity governor, what the machine is
*for*.

# II. THE PROTAGONIST — ASHER FORESTER

*(Canon root: reference board synopsis; Book I §IV.6. Everything below is binding for
writers and the VO director.)*

## II.1 The sheet

**Age 34. Born 2037**, in Halden's Ford, a Ledger town on the redwood coast — inside what
will become Concession III. His childhood is the last generation to remember the coast
*loud*: he is old enough to have heard a real dawn chorus and young enough to doubt the
memory.

**Family.** Father: Roy Forester, harvest-crew foreman from the first commercial Bioharvests
— a company man who drank his evenings quiet and taught his son that *the work is the work*.
Died 2062; Bright Hour service, urn niche in an arcology columbarium — no tree. Mother:
Enna, Ledger parishioner; died 2059; her Returning was held at the Halden's Ford Standing
Hall and her son, sixteen and newly pinned as an Edge Scholar, did not attend — he had an
assessment exam, and his father told him she'd understand. **The unattended funeral is
Asher's load-bearing guilt**; the campaign's second act will hold a Returning he does attend,
and the writing must let those two funerals rhyme without a single line pointing at it.

**Education & career.** Edge Scholar pin at fourteen (the family's pride and its mortgage —
Book I §V.3). Wardenry Academy, Cadence. Twelve years of service, six in Amber. Warden First
Class; two commendations; the Amber Incident (2068): three workers dead under a D-2's
cutting head, one survivor — Corporal Dez Arno — dragged clear by Asher at the cost of his
left hand. Ivory Meridian-pattern prosthetic, amber status ring, Edge property clause in the
warranty. Defected 2070.

**The secret** (revealed Act II, Chapter 5 — the campaign's central personal revelation):
hours before the Amber Incident, Asher himself flagged the night crew's permit anomaly in a
routine report. His filing is what reclassified the crew "unauthorized." His paperwork is
why the D-2's threat-table contained them at all. The machine took his hand; **his
correctness took three lives.** Edge buried the report chain in the settlement. Asher has
never said it aloud. Mara Okafor has read it — Sower forgers pulled the file during his
vetting — and recruited him anyway, and has been waiting two years for him to say it first.

**Philosophy at start:** *"The work is neutral. Harm is a failure of procedure."* The
Wardenry creed with the serial numbers filed off. His arc is the demolition of that
sentence, clause by clause.

**Strengths:** procedural mastery (he plans sabotage like a shift supervisor, which is why
it works); total physical competence; the specific credibility of the defector — he knows
the rotations because he wrote some of them. **Weaknesses:** obeys structure under stress
(early-game flaw with mechanical expression: he clears rooms Wardenry-style, announces
compliance phrases under pressure); cannot ask for help; self-punishing — treats his own
comfort as an accounting error. **Fears:** the sigh — a Converter cascade anywhere in
earshot fires phantom pain through the missing hand (audio-design directive: the player
*feels* this as controller rumble and a low-frequency swell every time, all game); being
recognized by Wardens he trained; greenhouses (he can't say why; his mother kept one).

**Habits.** Cleans and re-stows equipment as self-soothing; eats standing up; keeps the
gifted redwood seedling — planted in an inverted Vault lid — and speaks to it **exactly one
sentence a day**, never more. (Motif: the daily sentences are written into the script, one
per chapter, and they are the truest dialogue in the game. Chapter 1: *"Still here."* Final
scene: see §IX.)

**Speech style.** Clipped, procedural, present-tense. Radio grammar under stress. Dry
gallows humor that only surfaces sideways — he is funny the way exhausted professionals are
funny, deadpan and self-directed. Goes silent when moved; the script gives him *fewer* lines
at emotional peaks, never more. He never swears at people, only at objects.

**Contradictions.** Hates Edge with a company man's precision. Defected on principle but
still stands when a Wardenry officer enters a room — the body remembers. Carries the Ledger
in his bones and believes none of it, until he does.

**Dream imagery** (recurring, evolving — see Motifs §XV): the forest above Halden's Ford,
alive, while the sigh plays somewhere out of sight; each act the dream's grey line advances,
then in the epilogue the dream is replaced — for the first time — by a memory.

**Reason to continue,** in his own eventual words, at Sela's Returning: *"I filed a report
once. I'm correcting it."*

## II.2 The arc

Instrument → witness → author. Act I: he executes other people's plans perfectly and calls
it penance. Act II: his secret detonates; the cell's trust breaks; he learns that expiation
is just procedure wearing grief's coat — and that Mara knew, and the ledger does not need
him innocent, it needs him *planting*. Act III: he authors the plan himself — including its
cost, including the blackout, including what he does at the governor — and stands in the
consequence with his name on it. The fundamental change: a man who begins the game asking
*"what are my orders"* ends it as the only person in the room willing to decide.

# III. THE MAIN ANTAGONIST — DR. BECK GRIMWOOD

*(Canon root: reference board character sheet; Book I §IV, §VIII. The board's design is
final: the split silhouette — clean pale coat, ruined mechanical arm, brass-rimmed round
goggles, the collar mask.)*

## III.1 The man

**Born 2001**, Halden's Ford — *the same town as Asher*, two generations apart; the campaign
withholds this until Chapter 10, and it must never be played as a twist, only as a
confirmation the attentive player has already assembled from environmental evidence (both
men's Chargehand marginalia use the same provincial school-hand; the parish ledger in the
Evergreen chamber lists both families on one page). Son of the parish Reckoner. Raised
keeping the orchard-cemetery's books by hand.

**The wound.** 2013: a root blight takes the Halden's Ford orchard in one season — the
grave-trees of four hundred years, his mother's among them. The parish prays, plants, and
loses everything anyway. Twelve-year-old Beck watches his father — the town's accountant of
being — re-inscribe four centuries of names into a new ledger *for trees that no longer
existed*, and concludes, with a child's terrible clarity: **planting is sentiment; banking
is love.** *"My father returned everything to a ledger that never paid him back. I will
build one that does."* (This sentence is carved, in his adult hand, above the Evergreen
governor. It is the last environmental text the player reads before the final choice.)

**Career:** Book I §IV.3–IV.7 — the 2031 paper, the Converter, Edge, the Golden Decade, the
suppressed rotation truth, the Devastator program, seclusion after 2069. Add the private
chronology: he re-derived Lange's 2053 soil findings *independently, earlier,* and said
nothing — not to protect Edge's stock, but because the finding confirmed his deepest premise
(the wild cannot be sustained, only archived) and he had already begun designing Evergreen.
The century's greatest crime, the memo, was to Grimwood merely *redundant paperwork*.

**Compassion, love, regret.** He funds, anonymously and lavishly, the Republic's burn wards
and the provincial water grid. He has memorized the species list of his childhood orchard —
all of it, Latin binomials, four hundred entries — and recites it privately the way other
men pray; the player can overhear the end of the recitation in Evergreen's antechamber, and
the audio direction is *tenderness, not menace*. He keeps Asher's Amber Incident file on his
desk not as leverage but because Asher is the only living person Grimwood believes has paid
a fair personal price for the harvest — he regards the ex-Warden, sincerely, as a colleague
in grief. His regret is singular and structural: *that the world made his solution
necessary.* He has never once regretted the solution.

**Speech style.** Soft, unhurried, precise; the vocabulary of stewardship, never of
extraction — he says *returning, keeping, devotion, estate*; he has not said the word
"profit" in forty years. Never raises his voice, including while ordering violence, which he
phrases as maintenance. VO direction: cast warmth. The horror is the warmth.

**Symbols, architecture, music.** His mark is the double rule — the Ledger's
balance-struck — which he has redrawn with the second line *thicker than the first*: the
account closed by authority rather than by return. His space is the Evergreen chamber
(Chapter 10): a **Standing Hall inverted** — the same nave proportions as his father's
church, poured deep underground, aperture facing *down* into the machine, light rising cold
from below, and where the altar-tree should stand, the array. Composers: his theme is the
Ledger's plainsong hymn re-voiced for solo piano over grid-hum, always slightly *slower*
than the sacred original — reverence with the tempo of embalming.

**Followers.** Evergreen's staff are not thugs; they are the Republic's best ecologists,
archivists, and Bright Hour registrars, every one of whom joined to *save nature* and can
defend the position in front of the player — several do, in optional dialogue that is
deliberately hard to argue with.

**Downfall and legacy:** §IX. He is never killed by the player. Something worse and truer
happens: he is *audited*.

## III.2 Why the player almost agrees

The campaign gives Grimwood the three strongest scenes in the script (Chapters 6, 9, 10) and
plays fair: his models are correct as far as models go; his archive genuinely is the only
plan on the table that scales; the Sowers' arithmetic genuinely does not close. The design
intends a specific, uncomfortable player-state entering the finale: *I cannot beat this man
in the argument. I can only out-live him in the verb.* The refutation of Grimwood is not a
speech — no speech in the script attempts it — it is the valley (§IX), and even then the
game concedes his half: one valley. He banked a hemisphere; the reversal bought one green
valley and a blacked-out city. The ending forces the player to feel that *both* numbers are
real.

# IV. SECONDARY ANTAGONISTS — FOUR READINGS OF "KEEPING ALIVE"

Every boss and named opponent embodies one answer to the Central Question, so that the
campaign's combat is the argument conducted by other means.

## IV.1 Commander Edda Harrow — *order is what's kept alive*
Wardenry regional commander, Concession III; **Asher's academy instructor and the nearest
thing he has had to family since 2062.** Sixty-one, provincial-born like most of her rank,
the author of half the field doctrine Asher still moves by. She believes the creed —
*"between the work and harm"* — as only its author can: the Wardenry, to Harrow, is the thin
disciplined line that keeps the harvest from becoming a massacre, and every Sower success
makes Edge fund more Devastators and fewer humans, which is to say: less restraint. Her
argument to Asher, and it is not weak: *"Every tree you save costs me ten men's jobs and
buys a machine that saves nothing."* Her arc is the collapse of the creed's middle: ordered
in Act II to raid the Standing Hall (Chapter 6), she executes the order with perfect
professional restraint — and the orchard burns anyway, on camera, because restraint was
never the variable. Edge retires her within the week; the optics needed a name. **Boss
encounter:** Chapter 6, the Hall's colonnade — an honor-structured duel she initiates so
that her Wardens *won't* pursue the survivors; the fight is her resignation letter. She
survives it (the player "wins" by outlasting, not killing), surrenders her sidearm to the
burned nave's altar-stump, and walks out of the faction and the story — until the epilogue,
where she is glimpsed once, in provincial clothes, planting. Some enemies forgive
themselves; the script lets her try.

## IV.2 Unit D-2 / C3-0881, "the Amber machine" — *the institution is what's kept alive*
The quadruped siege-harvester from the Amber Incident — repaired, re-certified, returned to
service, because scrapping a nine-figure asset over three deaths was never on any agenda.
It is not haunted; it is not aware; **that is the horror the boss is built to carry.** The
player meets it again in Chapter 5, defending the largest regrowth event on record, and the
fight is staged so its threat-table logic is legible — it prioritizes the sapling over the
humans, exactly as designed, exactly as in 2068. Arena: the stillwood cathedral where
Asher's crew died, the old cordon tape still on the trees. Mid-fight, Harrow's voice on the
Wardenry band reads Asher's own 2068 permit report aloud — the secret detonated not as a
cutscene but as combat barks while the machine that executed his paperwork tries to finish
the job. Its death is the campaign's loudest catharsis and its emptiest: the machine does
not know it is dying, the sigh of its capacitors venting is indistinguishable from a
harvest, and the kill changes nothing at Edge — they book the loss and schedule a D-3.
What changes is Asher, who says the truth aloud for the first time over the wreck.

## IV.3 Director Coren Voss — *the present is what's kept alive*
Edge's operational face (Book I §XIV.1). Sixty-four; grid engineer by training, the man who
kept the hospitals lit through the 2064 Crash and has never let anyone forget it — least of
all himself, because it is the one unambiguously good thing he has done and he knows it.
Voss thinks Grimwood is a mystic and Evergreen is a cathedral built on the operating
budget: banked charge pays no bills; the grid needs the canopy *sold*, not archived. **He is
not a boss; he is a negotiation** (Chapter 9, Steelgate, after the Devastator-3000 falls):
Voss offers Asher the deal the player will be tempted by — *give me Grimwood, and Edge
cancels Evergreen; the canopy stands; the Concessions continue; the lights stay on; nothing
fundamental changes.* It is the Accord Age itself speaking: survival now, priced in
everything later. The script requires the player to refuse *actively* (walk past the offered
hand) — the game's only door that must be pushed. His fate: the epilogue's Edge belongs to
Voss — the corporation survives by devouring its founder, and the sequel's antagonist
landscape is a monopoly run by a man with no dream at all, which the franchise regards as
worse.

## IV.4 The Devastator-3000 — *the work is what's kept alive*
The board's red giant; the Battle of Steelgate's set piece (canon). No psychology, by
design — it is the Accord Age's answer rendered in alloy: continuation without content, the
forklift at the end of history. Its narrative function is architectural: Edge parks it in
Steelgate's lobby beneath the etched 2038 stock chart, and the fight demolishes the chart —
the player literally brings the graph down on the machine that enforced it. (Full boss
staging: §XIII.)

# V. SUPPORTING CAST

*(Format: role — person — arc — ending. Every named character owns a want, a fear, and a
relationship that changes. Deaths are earned; disappearances are recorded; nobody is
furniture.)*

**MARA OKAFOR — the mentor.** 52; ex-Republic ranger service (Concession III district chief
until the service was defunded under the ninety-nine-year lease); founding member of cell
Taproot; the "critical friend" of the board's synopsis — she gave Asher the seedling, and
she gave it to him *as a test she hoped he'd fail differently.* Botanist's patience, chess
player's ruthlessness about time. Philosophy: planter-wing pragmatism — *"a dead Sower
plants nothing."* Fear: that she has become a curator of losses — that the cells are the
Ledger's hospice, not its army. Her relationship to Asher is the story's spine: recruiter →
handler → the person who knew his secret all along → finally, at Sela's Returning, something
like family. Ending: alive; leads the valley's first parish. Her arc's cost: authorizing the
Steelgate operation means adopting the demolition wing's methods she spent five years
resisting — the script makes her say the order plainly and live with it on camera.

**COIL (Kestrel Ondo) — the machine specialist.** 26; ex-Mulcher salvage prodigy from
Driftmark; builds and maintains the cell's reverse-cycled "green units," including the one
she grafts onto Asher's stolen Converter. The campaign's humor lives here — shop-floor,
fast, unsentimental — and its youngest grief: she has never heard a wild bird and refuses,
loudly, to consider this sad, which is how the player knows it is. Want: one original
thing — every object in her life is salvage, including, she suspects, her convictions.
Relationship: adopts Asher as a bench partner; teaches his ivory hand to hold a soldering
iron; names the reversed Converter's ascending chord "the anti-sigh" and pretends she
didn't say it tenderly. Ending: alive; the epilogue implies (never states) that the banned
graphic novel's most-copied pages are inked in a salvage-marker style the player has seen
all game on her toolboxes. Sequel thread, held open (§XVII).

**DR. IVY LANGE — the scientist.** 58; author of the suppressed 2053 soil memo (Book I
§IV.5); Grimwood's last collaborator to leave. Asher's guilt-mirror — she filed a truthful
document into a machine that made it a weapon of delay, and has spent eighteen years
watching the filing matter more than the finding. Provides the campaign's factual
revelations (Chapters 4, 6) and its coldest line, at the Furrows: *"You think the crime was
hiding the memo. The crime was that a memo was the form the truth had to take."* Ending:
survives; testifies before the Green Courts in the epilogue with the Registry's numbers —
the world's slow lawful turn beginning, one bench at a time.

**RECKONER SELA IMMEN — the religious leader.** 74; keeper of the Concession III Standing
Hall and its seed-vaulted orchard of names; ordained the year the first Converter shipped.
She is not gentle-old-priest stock: she is an accountant of being with a ledger voice and
no patience for either Edge's arithmetic or the Sowers' romance. To Asher she gives the
campaign's absolution-that-isn't: *"The ledger doesn't need you innocent. It needs you
planting."* **She dies in Chapter 6** — not heroically staged but procedurally: she refuses
to leave the burning orchard's books, and the smoke takes her while she carries the third
volume out; the volumes survive; the image of a Warden carrying her body out of the smoke —
Dez Arno, out of uniform — becomes the banned graphic novel's most reproduced page and the
war's moral turning. Her Returning (Chapter 7's opening) is the funeral Asher finally
attends; the parish plants her beneath the burned Hall's one surviving sapling.

**TOMAS VERGE — the architect.** 81; Gravity Style master; poured Steelgate's deep
foundations in the 2040s and the Evergreen chamber's shell in the 2060s, believing to the
last drawing that he was building the Republic's memory. The campaign's walking argument
that *architecture is complicity with whoever owns the door.* Lives alone in an unheated
wing of his own unfinished civic commission in Cadence, drafting corrections to buildings
no one will amend. Provides the breach route (Chapter 7) — not from blueprints, which Edge
holds, but from memory, which they never confiscated: *"I know where the concrete is
honest."* Ending: disappears — he walks the player to the deep-pour access, hands over his
site lantern, and declines to come; the epilogue shows his commission finally finished by
the city, to his corrected drawings, unattributed. He would have preferred it that way; the
script never says so.

**REGISTRAR OREN PALE — the historian.** 44; Bright Hour registrar, Cadence Third Chapel;
the believer whose faith required accurate books (Book I §XIV.6). Copied Edge manifests
nightly for eleven years, did the arithmetic in year nine, kept copying — because a record
half-kept is a record lost. Brings the Registry's duplicate books to the Sowers in Chapter
8; his crisis is not losing faith but *re-deriving* it: if the harvest was never a
translation, then every lit window he blessed is a debt, and a registrar's duty in a debtor
faith is disclosure. Ending: disappears into the Registry with the books; his final scene
is a hand-off at a mag-platform, his line the franchise's quiet thesis: *"I don't save
worlds. I keep accounts. Someone has to be believed later."*

**BRACK MERON — the explorer, the betrayer, the forgiven.** 49; Driftmark salvage captain;
moves the cell's seed and steel through the drowned suburbs. Sells the cell's Standing Hall
route to the Wardenry in Chapter 6 — not for wealth: for **permit amnesty for his crews**,
whose names Harrow's office held over him; the writing makes the ledger of his betrayal
legible and small and human, eleven livelihoods against one church, and lets the player
feel how reasonable it was. The raid he enables kills Sela. His Act III: comes back anyway,
uninvited, and opens Driftmark's smuggler locks for the Steelgate approach, knowing what he
is owed. **The player chooses** (§XVI): Asher's forgiveness, or Asher's refusal — Mara
forgives him either way, on the Ledger's grounds (*"debts are planted, not carried"*), and
the choice alters only the epilogue's last Driftmark vignette: who stands beside whom at
the tide-line. Ending: alive, either way; some accounts stay open.

**CORPORAL DEZ ARNO — the former enemy.** 31; the survivor Asher pulled from under the D-2
in 2068 (canon); still serving in 2071 because leaving the Wardenry means leaving its
medical coverage, and his lungs never recovered from Amber. Meets the player as an enemy
squad leader in Chapter 3 — scripted encounter, spared or bypassed (the campaign's first
relationship choice). Carries Sela's body out of the smoke in Chapter 6, out of uniform;
defects that night, bringing the Wardenry's Steelgate rotation schedules (Chapter 8). His
want: to pay Asher back and be released from the debt; his fear: that he was saved by
paperwork's author, which makes his life a rounding error in the same ledger that killed
his crewmates — the script gives him the campaign's angriest scene, directed *at* Asher,
and lets it stand unanswered. Ending: alive; the epilogue's strongest implication about the
graphic novel's anonymous artist points here — the Amber Incident thumbnails are drawn from
inside the cordon (§XVII holds the thread).

**NOOR ESTEVEZ — the survivor.** 60s; the last resident of Halden's Ford; waters the
parish orchard's four surviving grave-trees by bucket from a mineral river, daily,
uninstructed, for nine years. Optional character (Chapter 5's stillwood approach); no
quest, no combat; a door the player can knock on. She feeds the player, shows them the
ledger she cannot read (she was Bright Hour; she keeps it anyway), and asks one question —
*"You're Roy's boy?"* — that lands harder than any boss. Ending: the epilogue's valley
regrowth reaches Halden's Ford's watershed; the final optional scene of the game is her
turning off her alarm clock, because the river sounds different now.

**JUNE — the child.** 9; Driftmark dock kid; trades salvage and *sounds* — owns a working
2040s field recorder with one intact cartridge: a dawn chorus, forty minutes, the coast
before the Quiet. Sells listens for scrip; gives Asher his listen free because his hand is
"the good kind of robot." The campaign's index of what is actually at stake — not
hemispheres: *whether June's category "bird" ever gets a referent.* Her recorder is the
score's diegetic anchor (the epilogue needle-drop, §IX). Ending: alive; the last shot of
the epilogue belongs to her (§IX).

# VI. THE RELATIONSHIP MAP

*(Full pair-wise matrix maintained in the writers' room; the load-bearing edges below. Every
edge is written to move at least once.)*

- **Asher ↔ Mara:** recruit/handler → the secret's custodian revealed → chosen family. The
  campaign's spine; its stress test is Chapter 5 (she knew) and its proof is Chapter 7 (he
  stays anyway).
- **Asher ↔ Grimwood:** enforcer/founder who have never met → mirrored sons of Halden's
  Ford → the governor's two hands. They share exactly two scenes and one town; the script
  builds the relationship almost entirely through environment (§XII) so their Chapter 10
  meeting feels like a reunion the player can't source.
- **Asher ↔ Harrow:** cadet/instructor → hunter/quarry → the duel as mutual resignation.
  The parental echo is deliberate and unspoken; she is the second parent whose funeral he
  refuses to attend — this time by sparing her.
- **Asher ↔ Dez:** savior/saved inverted by the secret — the man he saved is the man his
  report endangered. Anger → labor alongside → a debt neither can close, converted (in the
  Ledger's grammar) into something plantable.
- **Asher ↔ Coil:** the hand. She rebuilds its firmware, teaches it fine work, and by
  Chapter 8 the prosthetic that was Edge's receipt has become her masterpiece — the object's
  ownership migrates across the campaign without a line of dialogue about it.
- **Mara ↔ Sela:** the movement's two mothers — strategy and scripture — twenty years of
  argument about whether the Sowers are an army or a parish. Sela's death ends the argument
  by making both true.
- **Grimwood ↔ Voss:** dreamer and operator, Book I's "secrecy vs. scale" fault line made
  personal: Voss funds Evergreen to control Grimwood and Grimwood staffs it to outlive
  Voss. Their break (Chapter 9) is the campaign's institutional climax.
- **Grimwood ↔ Lange:** the two people who knew in 2053. He classified her memo *approvingly*
  — her finding was his premise. She has spent eighteen years as the living person his
  archive cannot file.
- **Brack ↔ everyone:** the price of the neutral zone. His edges are all debts: crews
  (protected), Mara (owed), Sela (unpayable), Asher (player-set).

# VII. NARRATIVE STRUCTURE — THE CAMPAIGN

*(Prologue, three acts, epilogue; eleven chapters. Each chapter block below is the
production unit: writers, level design, environment art, audio, and cinematics all key off
these entries. GN = graphic-novel sequence, per §XI.)*

## PROLOGUE — "THE SIGH" *(2068; playable memory)*
**Purpose:** establish tone, scale, combat grammar, and the wound — as a Warden. **Location:**
Concession III night harvest, redwood coast. **Emotional objective:** competence → horror;
the player must *enjoy* being good at the job for twenty minutes so the ending of the
prologue costs them something. **Gameplay:** Warden escort duties — perimeter, spotlights,
crew protection; the tutorial teaches systems as *procedure* (the HUD is Edge-liveried;
compliance phrases are UI). **Revelations withheld:** the permit report exists on-screen for
four seconds as routine paperwork the player themselves files — the campaign's biggest
secret is planted as a tutorial prompt. **Set piece:** the Incident — D-2 reclassification,
the cutting head, the hand; smash to white. **GN-1** carries 2068→2071 (the settlement, the
prosthetic, the plant on the windowsill, the duffel bag) in eleven wordless pages.
**Hook out:** title card over the stillwood at grey noon; the wordmark's final E grows one
green line (canon title screen, Book I App. C).

## ACT I — "DEBIT"

### Chapter 1 — "Grey Noon"
**Purpose:** the world of 2071; the cell; the covenant of the wave. **Location:** stillwood
traversal → Taproot cell nest (a Sower warren inside a dead Accord viaduct) → first regrowth
site. **Emotional objective:** isolation → provisional belonging. **Gameplay objective:**
reach Taproot; then the first **regrowth defense** — the canonical wave encounter: a
sapling, a reversed Converter charging, Edge survey drones then a harvest team inbound;
hold until the charge banks. **Environmental storytelling:** the stillwood as cathedral
(Book I §VI.2's rhyme, played fully); Chargehand glyphs annotating the dead tract like
marginalia; the cell's walls papered with seed-packet letters (§XIV). **Character:** Mara's
test — she hands him a spade before she hands him a weapon; Coil re-governs his stolen
Converter on-camera. **Collectibles:** Wardenry patrol logs (his old sector; two are signed
A.F.). **Cutscene:** none — the chapter is deliberately uncut; the first GN waits.
**Hook:** the banked sapling *survives the night* — dawn over green, forty seconds,
no UI, no music but wind. The player's first credit entry.

### Chapter 2 — "The Orchard of Names"
**Purpose:** the Ledger; the moral floor of the war. **Location:** the Concession III
Standing Hall, its orchard-cemetery, Sela's seed vault. **Emotional objective:** awe →
tenderness; the game's first *shelter* beat (Book I §VI.3 rationing). **Gameplay:** escort
seed crates through stillwood; defense of the orchard against a Mulcher poaching crew
(introducing the neutral faction as ambiguous, not evil). **Revelations:** the Returning
rite shown, not explained; Asher's unattended funeral surfaces in *one* line and is
dropped. **Environmental storytelling:** grave-trees with name-tags grown into bark; the
deconsecrated wing where stillwooded grave-trees stand — the parish's second cemetery, the
one nobody visits. **Cutscene (in-engine, 90s):** Sela reads the parish ledger aloud at
lamplight — births, plantings, withdrawals — bookkeeping as liturgy. **Hook:** Edge's
public broadcast: **Project Evergreen announced** — "the end of wild harvesting." The Hall
watches the feed in silence; Sela closes the ledger; Mara looks at Asher. GN-2: the
broadcast re-cut as the banned comic's satirical spread.

### Chapter 3 — "Driftmark"
**Purpose:** the world's grey market; the cost ledger of neutrality; June. **Location:**
the drowned suburbs, tide markets, Brack's yards. **Emotional objective:** curiosity →
warmth → first suspicion (Brack's crews' permit trouble is visible in background detail
two chapters before it matters). **Gameplay:** Vault convoy heist through half-tide
concrete; first Wardenry human squads — led by **Dez Arno**; the spare/bypass choice.
**Character:** June's forty-minute cartridge; Asher buys a listen with the truth ("I heard
one once. Real one."); Coil's homecoming swagger and the salvage-marker style on every
surface she's ever owned. **Collectibles:** pre-Retreat house interiors, water-stained
family registries — the First Spending's own orchard of names. **Boss:** none. **Cutscene
(30s, held frames):** the stolen Vaults opened in the nest — teal light on faces; Mara:
*"Six months of nights."* **Hook:** Lange arrives at Taproot uninvited, carrying a
seventeen-year-old memo and the sentence that ends the act's innocence: *"Evergreen isn't
a farm. I've seen the intake schedule. It's a funeral."*

## ACT II — "WITHDRAWAL"

### Chapter 4 — "The Furrows"
**Purpose:** the machine that feeds the cities; Lange's guilt; revelation one. **Location:**
a kilometer-long greenhouse vault — the game's brightest, greenest space, and every leaf
property of Edge. **Emotional objective:** wonder inverted into unease (abundance as
evidence). **Gameplay:** stealth-forward infiltration to Lange's archived lab partition;
wave defense at the exfil dock. **Revelation 1:** the 2053 memo chain — rotation failure
known, filed, *approved for silence* by a signature the player will not recognize until
Chapter 6 re-shows it (Grimwood's, initialed in Chargehand). **Environmental storytelling:**
the Furrows' worker shrines — Bright Hour filament-circles zip-tied to irrigation pipes;
the corporate chapel with attendance tracked. **Cutscene (60s):** Lange at her old bench,
not touching anything. *"You think the crime was hiding the memo…"* **Hook:** the intake
schedule decrypts: Concession III, Amber. The canopy. *His* coast. Ninety days.

### Chapter 5 — "Amber" — **the campaign's hinge**
**Purpose:** homecoming; the Amber machine; the secret detonates. **Location:** Halden's
Ford stillwood → the 2068 incident site → the record regrowth event beyond it (a living
acre, impossible, loud). **Emotional objective:** dread → catharsis → shame — in that
order, on a timer. **Gameplay:** the approach is optional-encounter rich (Noor's door);
then the **D-2 boss** at the regrowth acre — three phases across the old cordon (§XIII).
**Revelation 2 (personal):** mid-fight, Harrow on the open band reads the 2068 permit
report, author's serial included; the cell hears it; *Dez, monitoring Wardenry traffic,
hears it.* **Character:** post-fight, over the machine's venting wreck, Asher says it
plainly to Mara — and learns she has always known. The scene is played standing apart, no
touch, no absolution; her line is logistics: *"Debrief's at six. Bring the spade."*
**Collectibles:** the cordon's evidence tags, still zip-tied, 2068 dates. **GN-3:** the
Incident redrawn — the same eleven pages as GN-1, two panels different: what the artist
could not have seen unless they were inside the cordon. (Thread, not text.) **Hook:**
Wardenry mobilization order intercepted: the Standing Hall.

### Chapter 6 — "The Standing Hall"
**Purpose:** the war's moral turn; Sela; Harrow's creed collapses; revelation three.
**Location:** the Hall and orchard, night, then fire. **Emotional objective:** loss —
staged with the game's harshest restraint: no slow-motion, no score until the last minute,
smoke physics doing the grieving. **Gameplay:** evacuation defense (waves with a civilian
throughput objective — the design must make *carrying books* a combat verb); then the
**Harrow duel** in the colonnade. **Events:** Brack's betrayal surfaces in the Wardens'
route knowledge; Sela dies carrying volume three; Dez carries her out, out of uniform, and
does not go back. **Revelation 3:** among Sela's rescued volumes, the Halden's Ford parish
ledger — the Forester plantings and, forty pages earlier, in a boy's provincial
school-hand, the Grimwood family's. The player is allowed to find this; no character
comments. **Cutscene (2m, the act's spine):** the orchard burning in wide static frames —
Gravity Style framing applied to trees; Harrow watching her restraint mean nothing.
**Hook out:** GN-4 — *the page*: Dez and Sela, smoke, ink; caption-less; the Republic's
feeds pick it up by morning. The Quiet, for one news cycle, becomes visible.

### Chapter 7 — "The Architect" *(Act II epilogue / Act III overture)*
**Purpose:** grief metabolized into authorship; Verge; the plan. **Location:** Sela's
Returning at the burned Hall (playable, quiet — the funeral Asher attends); then Verge's
unfinished commission in Cadence. **Emotional objective:** mourning → resolve; the
campaign's stillest hour. **Gameplay:** none for twenty minutes by design (walking,
listening, one shovel); then the deep-pour reconnaissance with Verge's lantern.
**Character:** Asher's daily sentence to the seedling this chapter is the arc's pivot —
*"I'm not sorry anymore. I'm ready."* — and Mara authorizes Steelgate with the demolition
wing's methods, saying the order plainly. **Hook:** Voss moves Evergreen's schedule up.
Thirty days. The clock stops being a countdown and becomes a starting gun.

## ACT III — "RETURN"

### Chapter 8 — "Charge Day"
**Purpose:** the Accord Age at festival; the city the player is about to unlight; Pale and
Dez deliver the keys. **Location:** Cadence during the quarterly Charge Day — striplight
liturgy, Bright Hour choirs, the skyline as temple. **Emotional objective:** attachment —
deliberately: the player spends two hours among the people the blackout will cost, June's
recorder scene included (she has never heard her own cartridge on real air; Coil rigs a
speaker; forty minutes of 2040s dawn over a Driftmark dock at night; nobody in the scene
speaks). **Gameplay:** three-thread infiltration prep (Pale's books, Dez's schedules,
Brack's locks — the betrayer's uninvited return and the player's forgiveness choice).
**Environmental storytelling:** Charge Day's translation registers displayed publicly —
the tract-serials of "translated" forests read aloud as blessing; Asher hears Amber's
serials scheduled for next quarter's liturgy, pre-printed. **Hook:** the cell goes dark;
GN-5: eleven panels of gear checked in lamplight, the anti-sigh chord tuning up under the
final page.

### Chapter 9 — "Steelgate" — **the Battle of Steelgate (canon set piece)**
**Purpose:** the breach; the Devastator-3000; Voss's offer. **Location:** Edge HQ — the
lobby with the etched stock chart, the corridor cathedrals, the descending cores.
**Emotional objective:** awe weaponized — the Gravity Style turned hostile; then the cold
shock of the negotiation. **Gameplay:** the campaign's combat crescendo; the
**Devastator-3000 boss** under the chart (§XIII); then, in the silence after, **Voss** —
unarmed, coffee in hand, with the deal (§IV.3). The refusal is a walk, not a dialogue
wheel. **Revelation:** Voss's terms expose the Grimwood/Edge split — Evergreen was never
board-sanctioned at full scope; the founder has been building his ark inside their budget;
the corporation would happily trade its god for its graph. **Hook:** the deep-pour
elevator, Verge's lantern clipped to Asher's harness, descent. GN-6: one page, one panel —
the elevator shaft in section, eleven hundred meters of pour, one lamp.

### Chapter 10 — "Evergreen"
**Purpose:** the chamber; Grimwood; the governor. **Location:** the inverted Standing Hall
(§III.1) — nave proportions, downward aperture, the array where the altar-tree should be;
above the governor, carved: *"MY FATHER RETURNED EVERYTHING TO A LEDGER THAT NEVER PAID HIM
BACK. I WILL BUILD ONE THAT DOES."* **Emotional objective:** revelation → acceptance — the
almost-agreement engineered to peak here. **Gameplay:** the final encounter (§XIII): the
array's defense systems and the first live canopy-intake, fought while Grimwood speaks —
he never takes up a weapon; the fight is against his life's work, with him standing in it,
narrating it like a docent, reciting, near the end, the orchard species list. **The
choice-that-isn't-a-branch:** the governor, hand on polarity. Hold (bank the canopy —
Grimwood's Returning) or **reverse** (spend the Evergreen reserve back into the land — the
largest Return in history, ruinous at canon 10:1, and the grid pays tonight). The story
proceeds on reverse; the design frames it as the player's *authored* act — the input is
held, not tapped, ten full seconds, the anti-sigh building, Asher's knuckles white on the
lever his hand was built by the debtor to hold. **Grimwood's end:** not death — audit. He
watches four decades of banked verdance leave his ledger for the watershed, and his
composure finally moves — not to rage: to *arithmetic*; he begins recalculating aloud,
half-broken, half-liberated, as Voss's security arrives to arrest the founder whose ark
just spent the operating reserve. His last line to Asher, without bitterness: *"One
valley."* And Asher's answer, the campaign's thesis, quietly: *"One more than you were
leaving."*

## EPILOGUE — "THE FIRST CREDIT"
One year. The valley green and loud and *legally standing* — the Green Courts' first
injunction upheld on Registry numbers and one ungovernable image of a burning orchard;
precedent, the thing money cannot un-plant. Cadence remembers the Night the Lights Went
Out — eleven hours dark, hospitals on reserve (the script does not hide the harm: two
deaths in the blackout are read into the court record, and Asher hears them read); and
remembers also what stood in the dark: stars over the city, indecent and bright, for the
first time in a generation. Edge continues — Voss's Edge, dreamless and durable; the
Concessions continue; the Quiet is still a continent wide. One valley sings. Vignettes,
choice-inflected (§XVI): Harrow planting; Noor's alarm clock; Brack at the tide-line
(accompanied or alone); Lange testifying; Pale's books opening before the bench; Mara's
parish; Coil's marker drying on a page we do not see. Last scene: June, in the valley,
recorder raised out of habit — then lowering it, because the dawn chorus is *live*. She
lets the cartridge run out. Asher, planting the windowsill seedling at the tree line,
speaks his one sentence of the day — the game's final line, to the valley:
**"Still here."** Cut to the double rule, drawn thin and green. Credits in silence, then
wind, then — sixty seconds in — one bird.

# VIII. THE OPENING — THE FIRST THIRTY MINUTES, SHOT BY BEAT

Minute 0–2: black; wind with texture; the sigh, far off; one line of serif type — *CONCESSION
III · "AMBER" · 2068* — no logo, no menu (the menu is after the prologue, a deliberate
inversion). 2–6: the player walks a living forest at night as a Warden — rain on canvas,
crew chatter, spotlights through redwoods; the architecture of trees before the
architecture of concrete; tutorial as escort procedure. 6–12: harvest operations — the
player *runs a perimeter for a Converter crew*; the first sigh up close; the vault gauges
climbing; the game's central verb (protect) taught in the wrong livery. 12–14: the permit
prompt — a routine flag, two button presses, filed; the score does not react; nothing marks
it. 14–22: first combat — "unauthorized presence," moving lights, the escalation drill;
the player performs it correctly. 22–26: the reclassification cascade — the D-2 turns; the
crew is inside its table; procedure fails in exact accordance with procedure; the hand.
26–30: white; GN-1's eleven wordless pages (settlement, prosthetic, windowsill, duffel);
title card over grey noon; the green line grows through the E; *then* the main menu, which
is the stillwood, which now means something. The player exits the first half hour asking
every required question — where am I, what happened, who built this, who am I — with one
addition this franchise insists on: *what did I file?*

# IX. MIDGAME REVELATIONS & THE ENDING — DESIGN RATIONALE

**The revelation ladder** (each rung re-reads everything before it): R1 (Ch.4) — the
failure was known in 2053: the harvest was never sustainable and the Golden Decade was a
decision, not an era. R2 (Ch.5) — Asher's report: the player's tutorial keystrokes were the
crime; the game made them complicit thirty minutes in and tells them at hour twelve. R3
(Ch.6) — the parish ledger: antagonist and protagonist are the same town's two answers to
the same funeral. R4 (Ch.9) — Evergreen is Grimwood's, not Edge's: the corporation has no
dream at all, which is worse. R5 (Ch.10) — Evergreen *is* a Returning: the villain's plan
is the faith's holiest rite performed at civilization scale, sincerely. Every rung is
foreshadowed on-screen before it lands (the report in the tutorial; the school-hand in
marginalia; the budget anomalies in collectible manifests; the hymn tempo in the score).

**Why the ending is shaped this way.** No branch: the campaign's argument is that authorship,
not selection, is what the Accord Age abolished — so the finale is an authored act with a
held input, not a menu. No victory: the blackout kills two people and the script reads
their names, because a story about externalized costs cannot end by externalizing one. No
despair: the valley is real, legal, loud, and *disproportionate to the model* — hope with
evidence. The world continues (Voss's Edge, the standing Quiet); the protagonist's internal
conflict — the unattended funeral, the filed report — resolves in the only currency the
Ledger accepts: attendance and authorship. He was absent once and present now; he filed
harm once and files the Return now, signed. *"Still here"* closes both books at once.

# X. CINEMATIC PLAN (SELECTED; FULL SHOT LISTS IN BOOK VIII)

Conventions: in-engine; Gravity Style framing (humans <3% of frame height in establishing
shots); one light source per scene; dialogue sparse; runtime discipline brutal.

1. **Opening (GN-1, 2m10s):** graphic-novel pages, wordless; ink + one green. Purpose:
   compress two years of grief without a montage cliché. Transition: last panel's window
   becomes the menu's stillwood.
2. **Sela's Ledger (Ch.2, 90s):** single lamp, static camera, pages turning; the only
   cutscene in the game that is just reading aloud. Purpose: teach the faith by
   bookkeeping. Music: the plainsong hymn, correct tempo (plant the theme so Grimwood's
   slowed version lands later).
3. **Evergreen Announcement (Ch.2 tail, 45s):** the broadcast on a cracked hall screen —
   Edge's curated sunrise cut against the congregation's faces lit blue. No score.
4. **The Orchard Burns (Ch.6, 2m):** wide static frames; smoke as the only motion; Harrow
   small in her own shot. Music enters only for the final 20s — the hymn, broken to single
   notes. Purpose: loss without melodrama.
5. **The Page (GN-4, 40s):** one drawing assembling stroke by stroke — Dez and Sela in the
   smoke; the feeds picking it up as a wipe of reproductions. Purpose: show an image
   changing a Republic, the franchise's theory of media in one cut.
6. **Descent (GN-6 → Ch.10, 55s):** the elevator section drawing, one lamp, eleven
   kilometers; audio is cable-song and Asher's breathing; the anti-sigh tunes underneath.
7. **The Governor (Ch.10, in-engine, interactive-held):** not a cutscene — the held input
   IS the cinematic; camera slowly dollies from lever to face to aperture as the ten
   seconds build; Grimwood recites; the array's light shifts from harvest-white to green.
8. **Ending (4m):** the epilogue vignettes as slow pans; June's cartridge running out into
   live birdsong; the sentence; the double rule; credits per §VII. One bird at 0:60.

# XI. GRAPHIC-NOVEL SEQUENCE DESIGN (THE BANNED BOOK)

Canon (Book I §XI): cutscene interludes are pressed pages of the in-world banned graphic
novel *Echoes of Eden*, drawn by an anonymous Sower artist. Design rules: **two colors**
(ink-grey + one green; red permitted only in Devastator panels, per Book I palette law);
serif titles; silhouette-first compositions borrowed from the board's ideation sheets;
panel grammar is *held stillness* — motion between panels, never inside them (parallax and
light-shift only); text sparse to none (captions are Chargehand glyphs a reader slowly
learns); VO only where a document is being read in-world. Sound: paper texture, room tone,
the score's motif in its barest voicing. Sequence map: GN-1 defection (P→I), GN-2 the
announcement satire (Ch.2), GN-3 the Incident redrawn (Ch.5 — the two impossible panels),
GN-4 the page (Ch.6), GN-5 lamplight gear check (Ch.8), GN-6 the descent (Ch.9→10),
GN-7 epilogue frontispiece: the valley, drawn in advance of the pan that proves it. The
meta-rule: the player is reading the resistance's own record of events they lived —
including, twice, small discrepancies between what they did and what the book shows,
because records are made by people. (Those discrepancies are the artist-identity thread.)

# XII. ENVIRONMENTAL STORYTELLING — PER-CHAPTER LOAD-BEARING DETAILS

P: the crew's thermos culture, the permit terminal's worn keys — Edge's violence as office
work. Ch.1: Chargehand marginalia dating each tract's death; a ranger tower re-nested by
Sowers, its logbook continuous across the defunding — the state withered mid-sentence.
Ch.2: name-tags grown into bark; the deconsecrated grey wing; Sela's pencil amendments in
a printed hymnal. Ch.3: high-water lines inside nursery rooms; a pre-Retreat street sign
under three meters of tide, still legible at low water. Ch.4: worker shrines zip-tied to
irrigation; the chapel attendance ledger; one unauthorized tomato plant in a ventilation
duct, tended by nobody-admits-whom. Ch.5: the 2068 cordon tags; Noor's bucket path worn
into stone; Asher's childhood initials in a fence post the stillwood preserved. Ch.6: the
seed vault's triple locks opened by hand-me-down keys; the ledger volumes' fire-order
(Sela saved them oldest-first). Ch.7: Verge's corrected drawings nailed over the originals;
his bed in the unfinished nave. Ch.8: translation registers in public brass; Charge Day
bunting made from harvest-livery offcuts — festival as supply chain. Ch.9: the etched
stock chart floor-to-ceiling; a Wardenry locker with a child's tree drawing (Book I §I,
paid off); Voss's office: no art, one window, the grid. Ch.10: the inverted nave; the
species list in Grimwood's hand on four hundred index cards; the parish ledger open on
his desk to the Forester page. Epilogue: the valley's first informal path — desire lines,
the oldest architecture there is.

# XIII. BOSS NARRATIVES (STAGING DIGESTS)

**D-2 / C3-0881 (Ch.5).** Three phases across the 2068 cordon: (1) survey mode — it maps
the player as it once mapped the crew, spotlights and classification barks; (2) harvest
defense — it roots at the regrowth acre and the fight becomes a reversed wave (the player
*attacks* a defender); (3) cascade breach — capacitors venting, the sigh continuous, close
range, the cutting head. Harrow's report-reading rides phase 2. Music: percussion built
from sampled Converter clicks; no melody — machines don't get one. Death: it powers down
mid-gesture, unaware; the regrowth acre's birds resume before the players' pulse does.
Legacy: Edge books the loss; Asher speaks the truth over the wreck; the acre gets a name
(the parish names it for the three dead — environmental detail in the epilogue).

**Cmdr. Harrow (Ch.6).** Duel logic: colonnade, smoke light, her service carbine and
doctrine the player was trained by — she counters Wardenry-taught habits, forcing the
player to fight *unlike* Asher's past (design intent: mechanical apostasy). No health bar;
her armor plates shed visibly. She ends it standing, empties her sidearm into the
altar-stump's ash, and resigns in the same motion. Music: the creed's cadence drummed on
one snare, decelerating.

**Devastator-3000 (Ch.9).** The lobby as arena-as-argument: cover is monumental furniture;
the chart looms; the machine's phases escalate from asset-protection to structural
indifference — it wrecks the lobby it guards, because the work is what's kept alive.
Finale: the player drops the etched chart slab onto it — the graph kills the god's
bodyguard. Music: the campaign's only full-orchestra cue, cut dead at the collapse.

**Evergreen Array (Ch.10).** Not a health bar: a *schedule*. The intake runs; the player
denies it stations (waves + sabotage verbs) while Grimwood narrates; the "fight" ends not
in destruction but in reaching the governor. The array is beautiful — environment art is
directed to make the player hesitate — and the docent is kind. Music: the hymn at
Grimwood's tempo, resolving to the correct tempo only when the polarity flips.

# XIV. LORE SYSTEM (OPTIONAL DEPTH — NEVER REPEATS THE MAIN LINE)

Seed-packet letters (Sower dead-drop correspondence written on plantable paper); Wardenry
incident logs (the Quiet Belt's policing, deadpan); Lange's memo drafts 1→9 (watch the
language soften as it climbs the org chart — bureaucratic erosion as horror); Grimwood's
father's parish ledger (the blight year's entries, a child's hand appearing in the
margins); Bright Hour translation registers and one registrar's midnight arithmetic; the
children's primer *Where Does Light Come From?* (Edge-published, terrifying in retrospect);
June's cartridge catalog (labels only — the sounds are gone; the labels are poems);
Verge's correction drawings; Devastator maintenance tickets (D-2's 2068 repair order:
"cosmetic; return to service"); Mulcher tide-charts annotated with smuggler prayers;
Registry census margins recording the provinces' emptying in erasures.

# XV. EMOTIONAL PACING, DIALOGUE, MOTIFS

**Pacing map (by chapter):** P competence→horror · 1 isolation→belonging · 2 awe→tenderness
· 3 warmth→suspicion · 4 wonder→unease · 5 dread→catharsis→shame · 6 loss→anger · 7
mourning→resolve · 8 attachment→foreboding · 9 awe→cold clarity · 10
revelation→acceptance · E melancholy+hope, arriving together. No tone holds longer than
ninety minutes of play; every dark chapter contains one shelter beat; every warm chapter
contains one grey thread (Book I's rationing law, applied to feeling).

**Dialogue philosophy:** sparse; present-tense; no character explains the world to another
character who already knows it (the cardinal sin); silence is written into the script as
stage direction with durations; architecture finishes conversations (scenes are staged so
the reply to a hard question is a cut to what the space says). Every named character has
one sentence-shape the player could identify blind: Asher's clipped procedurals, Mara's
horticultural imperatives, Grimwood's stewardship clauses, Coil's salvage-compression,
Sela's double-entry cadences, Voss's balance-sheet conditionals, June's trade offers.

**Recurring motifs (tracked in the script database):** the sigh / anti-sigh (sound);
"Still here" (the daily sentence — eleven occurrences, evolving); the double rule (drawn
in dust at Taproot, in ash at the Hall, in wet concrete by Verge, in light at the end);
"What stands, remembers" (spoken of buildings in Ch.2, of the Wardenry in Ch.6, of the
stillwood in Ch.5, of the valley in E — the sentence's ownership migrates from concrete to
life); the ivory hand (receipt → tool → lure → the hand that holds the lever); the
windowsill seedling (pot = Vault lid: the harvest's container repurposed as a cradle — the
whole game in one prop); grey noon light vs. the single green frame-tenant; the parish
school-hand.

# XVI. PLAYER CHOICE (PERSPECTIVE, NOT ENDINGS)

Four instruments, none branching the finale: (1) Dez — spare/bypass in Ch.3 gates his Ch.8
defection scene's temperature and his epilogue vignette; (2) Brack — forgiveness at the
tide-line, altering the epilogue pairing and one GN panel; (3) Sela's Returning — the
player chooses Asher's spoken line from three truths (procedural, filial, Ledger), which
becomes the epigraph of GN-7; (4) optional doors (Noor, June's second listen, Verge's
drawings) — attendance changes what the epilogue has earned the right to show. Design law:
choices adjust *relationships and understanding*; the valley, the blackout, and the
governor are authored, not selected.

# XVII. SEQUEL THREADS (PLANTED, NEVER TEASED)

The artist's two impossible panels (who was inside the cordon?); Grimwood's genome archive
— spent of charge, *intact of record*, in Voss's inventory now; the other continents'
Concessions (Book I's hemisphere — the Republic was one theater); the regrowth events'
excess-over-model (the world keeping its own ledger — Book I §XII's closing line, now with
a valley of evidence); Harrow in provincial clothes, planting (the Wardenry's diaspora);
the Registry's books, opening one bench at a time; and the audited founder himself —
alive, arrested, recalculating: the franchise has not finished with the man who invented
the sigh.
