# SECTION 2 — WORLD SUMMARY (ONE PAGE)

In 2031 Dr. Beck Grimwood discovered that a living organism's accumulated metabolic energy
("verdance") can be withdrawn in minutes by a controlled cascade — clean, smokeless,
efficient. Commercialized by Edge Corporation from 2038 via the handheld **Converter**, the
Grimwood Cycle ended fossil fuels, lit the arcologies, and drank two-thirds of the planet's
wild biomass in one generation. Harvested land does not recover — the Cycle takes the soil
biome too, a fact known internally by 2053 and filed into silence. The dead forests remain
standing, grey and rigid: the **Stillwoods**, inside a spreading dead zone called **the
Quiet Belt**. The Meridian Republic's government remains sovereign in name but leased its
wildlands to Edge under the ninety-nine-year **Concession Acts** (2049– ); Edge's corporate
enforcers, the **Wardenry**, are the continent's largest armed force. Resistance is the
**Sowers** (founded 2066): nocturnal replanters running stolen Converters in reverse,
producing miraculous local **regrowth events**. The player is **Asher Forester**, 34,
ex-Warden First Class — maimed by a Devastator in 2068, radicalized by a gifted seedling,
and carrying a buried secret: his own routine report caused the deaths that cost him his
hand. The game races Edge's **Project Evergreen** — Grimwood's plan to drink the last great
living canopy whole and archive nature for a managed resurrection — and ends not with the
world saved but with one authored act: the Evergreen reserve spent back into the land, one
valley returned to life, one city dark for a night, and the first honest credit written
into a forty-year ledger of debits.

# SECTION 3 — UNIVERSE RULES (IMMUTABLE)

**Physics & technology**
1. One speculative license only: the Grimwood Cycle. All other technology obeys ordinary
   2070s-plausible physics. [BI §VII]
2. Cycle Law 1: only *living* systems hold verdance; dead biomass yields nothing;
   stillwoods cannot be re-harvested. [BI §VII.1]
3. Cycle Law 2: yield scales with accumulated growth (old growth = reservoir; a lawn =
   pocket change). [BI §VII.1]
4. Cycle Law 3: the cascade is reversible at ruinous efficiency (~10 spent : 1 banked);
   reversal accelerates growth in living/compatible tissue. [BI §VII.1]
5. **No AGI, ever.** Machines are exactly as moral as their owners. No sentient robots, no
   machine "wanting." [BI §VII.3]
6. No teleportation, no FTL, no energy weapons as standard arms — ballistics remain the
   sidearm truth; verdance powers grids, not handguns. [BI §VII.3]
7. Regrowth events exceed all models, including Grimwood's; the excess is never mechanically
   explained in game one. [BI §III.4; BII §XVII]

**World & politics**
8. The government and Edge are separate. The Republic let it happen and keeps letting it;
   it is never a puppet-with-strings cartoon. [BI §IX.1]
9. Edge is never mustache-twirling evil; every Edge believer can defend the position. [BI §XIV.1; BII §III]
10. The Quiet Belt's spread is not reversible at scale within game one; the Sowers'
    arithmetic never closes. [BII §I.1]
11. The collapse has no single villain year; it is cumulative and legible. [BI §0, §XII]

**Religion**
12. The Ledger forbids waste, not harvest; its sacrament is planting; Returning = burial
    beneath a grave-tree. [BI §VIII.1]
13. Wardens never enter a Standing Hall armed — standing order, held until Chapter 6, whose
    breaking is the war's moral turn. [BI §XIV.4; BII Ch.6]
14. The Bright Hour is sincere, sympathetic, and never mocked by the text. [BI §VIII.2]

**Narrative boundaries**
15. No chosen one, no save-the-world, no evil-AI, no aliens, no zombies. [BI §0; BII brief]
16. Asher never kills identified civilians; combat opponents are Wardenry, Edge machines,
    Mulcher combatants, and Evergreen defenses only. [BII passim]
17. Grimwood never raises his voice, never wields a weapon, and is never killed by the
    player. His end in game one: audited, arrested, alive. [BII §III, §VII Ch.10]
18. The ending is authored, not branched: the governor reversal, the blackout (11 hours,
    two deaths read into record), the one valley. Choices affect relationships and
    understanding only. [BII §IX, §XVI]
19. Dialogue is sparse; no character explains the world to another who already knows it. [BII §XV]
20. **Legacy NEON STRIKE gameplay content (maps, arcade enemy roster, MP modes) is
    NON-CANON for Echoes of Eden.** The codebase is a technical foundation only. [PROJECT_STATE]

**Visual law**
21. Green ≤ ~10% of any frame; red reserved for Devastator presence and alarm; one
    monumental light source per space. [BI §XVI]
22. Humans <3% of frame height in establishing shots (megalophobia rule). [BI §VI.2]
23. GN sequences: two colors (ink-grey + one green; red only in Devastator panels);
    motion between panels, never inside them. [BII §XI]

# SECTION 21 — CONTINUITY CHECK (FULL AUDIT, WITH RULINGS)

**A. Enna Forester's death date — CONTRADICTION (internal, BII §II.1).** Text gives
"Died 2059" AND "her son, sixteen and newly pinned." Born 2037 → sixteen = 2053; pin =
2051 ("newly," within two years). 2059 would make him 22 and seven years past the pin.
**RULING: Enna dies 2053; "2059" is struck.** Rationale: the scene's power depends on a
sixteen-year-old choosing an assessment exam; 2053 also lands the family grief in the
memo's year — two burials by filing, same year — which the Canon Office notes as earned
resonance, not addition. §4 and §7 already reflect the ruling.

**B. "Halden" name collision — INCONSISTENCY (BI §XIV.1 vs BII §II.1).** Book I named the
Director "Halden Voss"; Book II independently created the load-bearing town "Halden's
Ford." An unrelated shared name invites false reader inference. **RULING: the town stands
(it carries the Forester/Grimwood mirror); the Director's first name becomes **Coren
Voss**; "Halden Voss" is struck from BI §XIV.1.** Rationale: minimal-edit principle — the
town is structural, the first name is cosmetic.

plausible poured megastructure; preserves the image's vertigo without breaking Rule 1.
